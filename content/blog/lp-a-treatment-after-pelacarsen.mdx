Pelacarsen, the first drug designed to lower Lp(a) to complete a cardiovascular outcomes trial, did lower Lp(a), but it did not reduce cardiovascular deaths, heart attacks, strokes or urgent coronary procedures compared with placebo, Novartis announced on 4 September 2026.<Cite id="novartis-2026-horizon-topline" /><Cite id="healio-2026-pelacarsen" /> If your Lp(a) is high, what your number means has not changed: US guidelines still say to measure it once, and a high level is still linked to higher risk.<Cite id="nla-2026-horizon-statement" /><Cite id="aha-2026-dyslipidemia-guideline-news" /> The timeline is what changed. No drug is approved to lower Lp(a), and the next answers will come from four other outcome trials whose estimated completion dates run from 2028 to 2031.<Cite id="healio-2026-pelacarsen" /><Cite id="ctgov-ocean-outcomes" /><Cite id="ctgov-move-lpa" />

This page covers what the trial found, why the experts quoted since then say it does not settle the question for the next drugs, what lowers Lp(a) today, and what to do with a high result now. If you are new to the marker itself, start with [what a high Lp(a) means](/blog/lp-a-lipoprotein-a-high).

## What Lp(a)HORIZON tested, and what is known so far

The trial enrolled 8,323 adults aged 18 to 80 with established cardiovascular disease and an Lp(a) of at least 70 mg/dL. Established disease meant a heart attack or ischemic stroke between 3 months and 10 years before screening, or symptomatic peripheral artery disease.<Cite id="ctgov-lpa-horizon" /> Participants got an 80 mg injection of pelacarsen or a placebo once a month, on top of their usual lipid-lowering and blood pressure treatment.<Cite id="ctgov-lpa-horizon" /><Cite id="novartis-2026-horizon-topline" /> The primary endpoint combined cardiovascular death, non-fatal heart attack, non-fatal stroke and urgent coronary revascularization requiring hospitalization. It was tested in everyone and, separately, in people with Lp(a) of 90 mg/dL or higher.<Cite id="ctgov-lpa-horizon" />

What has been published so far fits in four lines:

- **Primary endpoint:** not met in the overall population.<Cite id="novartis-2026-horizon-topline" />
- **Lp(a):** "Consistent with previous studies, substantially lower lipoprotein (a) [Lp(a)] levels were achieved with pelacarsen," according to Ionis, which developed the drug. No percentage was given.<Cite id="ionis-2026-horizon-topline" />
- **Safety:** Ionis described "an acceptable safety profile."<Cite id="ionis-2026-horizon-topline" />
- **Not yet public:** the hazard ratio, the size of the Lp(a) drop, and the result in the 90 mg/dL subgroup. Novartis named that subgroup but reported no result for it.<Cite id="novartis-2026-horizon-topline" />

"These are not the results we hoped for," said Shreeram Aradhye, Novartis's chief medical officer.<Cite id="novartis-2026-horizon-topline" /> The full data are scheduled for the American Heart Association's Scientific Sessions, in the late-breaking session on Saturday 7 November 2026, as two presentations: the primary results and extended analyses of cardiovascular outcomes.<Cite id="aha-2026-late-breaking" /> The National Lipid Association noted that the full results "may yield further insight into treatment effect."<Cite id="nla-2026-horizon-statement" />

## Does this change what a high Lp(a) means for you?

The National Lipid Association says no. In its statement after the announcement, the National Lipid Association said Lp(a) "remains an important and independent risk factor" for atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, that "clinicians and patients should continue to measure Lp(a)," and that people with elevated levels should have their other risk factors managed intensively.<Cite id="nla-2026-horizon-statement" />

The association between Lp(a) and risk comes from population and genetic studies, not from drug trials. The 2022 European Atherosclerosis Society (EAS) consensus concluded that studies of hundreds of thousands of people support a causal and continuous link between Lp(a) concentration and cardiovascular outcomes.<Cite id="kronenberg-2022-eas-lpa-consensus" /> The 2026 ACC/AHA guideline puts numbers on it: an Lp(a) of 125 nmol/L or 50 mg/dL or higher is associated with about a 1.4-fold higher long-term risk of heart attack or stroke, and 250 nmol/L with at least a two-fold higher risk.<Cite id="aha-2026-dyslipidemia-guideline-news" />

Not everyone agrees. "Do we still need to measure Lp(a) in everyone? Another example of the guidelines getting ahead of the evidence," cardiologist David L. Brown posted on X.<Cite id="tctmd-2026-pelacarsen" /> In JAMA's news coverage, UCSF cardiologist Rita Redberg said the result is a chance "to question the importance of having outcomes data before we start embracing another measurement," and described seeing healthy people with a high Lp(a) and very low overall risk who were very anxious.<Cite id="abbasi-2026-jama-pelacarsen" /> Deepak L. Bhatt of Mount Sinai, who reports research funding from Novartis, framed the open question: "The key question is whether it is a modifiable mediator of risk, or merely a marker."<Cite id="healio-2026-pelacarsen" />

## Why experts say one result does not settle it for the other drugs

Until the full data are out, every explanation is a hypothesis. A review in The American Journal of Medicine in October lists the candidates: treatment after long cumulative exposure to Lp(a), already advanced artery disease, intensive background prevention, the size and duration of Lp(a) suppression, who was enrolled, their baseline risk, and biological differences between patients.<Cite id="sabouret-2026-horizon-disconnect" /> Here is what each argument rests on.

### How far Lp(a) came down

In phase 2, pelacarsen cut Lp(a) by an average of 72% at 60 mg every 4 weeks and 80% at 20 mg every week.<Cite id="tsimikas-2020-pelacarsen-phase2" /> The siRNA drugs went further in their phase 2 trials: placebo-adjusted reductions of 97% to 101% for olpasiran at its higher doses, 94% for lepodisiran, and 81% to 86% for zerlasiran.<Cite id="odonoghue-2022-olpasiran-phase2" /><Cite id="nissen-2025-lepodisiran-phase2" /><Cite id="nissen-2024-zerlasiran-phase2" /> These trials used different doses, time points and assays, so the figures are not a head-to-head comparison.

Why the gap might matter: genetic studies suggest that the benefit of lowering Lp(a) depends on the absolute drop in mg/dL, not the percentage. One estimated that about 100 mg/dL less Lp(a) would match the benefit of lowering LDL cholesterol by 38.67 mg/dL (1 mmol/L); another put the figure at about 66 mg/dL.<Cite id="burgess-2018-lpa-mendelian" /><Cite id="lamina-2019-lpa-effect-size" /> For scale, a 72% to 80% cut at the trial's minimum entry level of 70 mg/dL removes 50 to 56 mg/dL. Analysts at Citi wrote, "We would not declare the mechanism dead," adding that "[g]reater target suppression could matter if cardiovascular benefit requires crossing a biological threshold."<Cite id="fierce-2026-pelacarsen" />

### How the drug was given

Michelle O'Donoghue of Brigham and Women's Hospital, principal investigator of the olpasiran phase 3 trials, noted in JAMA that pelacarsen has a much shorter half-life than the siRNAs, which could have made Lp(a) levels fluctuate during the trial. She also suggested that giving it less often and at a higher dose than in phase 2 could have hurt tolerability.<Cite id="abbasi-2026-jama-pelacarsen" />

### Who was treated, and when

Everyone in the trial already had cardiovascular disease and was on guideline-directed treatment.<Cite id="novartis-2026-horizon-topline" /> Bhatt offered several possibilities, including "It could be the specific drug" and "It could be that Lp(a) levels were not high enough at baseline," and said strong LDL control may have left little room to show an extra benefit.<Cite id="healio-2026-pelacarsen" /> Donald Lloyd-Jones of Boston University said, "Maybe we need to start with a younger population and treat longer to really see an effect," and that differences between trial populations may matter more than differences between drugs.<Cite id="abbasi-2026-jama-pelacarsen" />

### Who stayed in the trial

François Mach, principal investigator at the trial's Geneva site, told JAMA that about half the participants at his site and at a colleague's site in Basel withdrew, and that because the trial was blinded he does not yet know whether they were mostly in the drug group.<Cite id="abbasi-2026-jama-pelacarsen" />

### Or lowering Lp(a) may simply not help

That remains possible, and the next trials are designed to answer it. Bhatt's view: "I strongly feel that the ongoing trials should continue until their planned end."<Cite id="healio-2026-pelacarsen" />

## The Lp(a) drugs still in trials

Pelacarsen is an antisense oligonucleotide; olpasiran, lepodisiran and zerlasiran are small interfering RNAs (siRNAs). All four reduce the liver's production of apolipoprotein(a), the protein that defines Lp(a). Muvalaplin is a daily pill that blocks the Lp(a) particle from forming.<Cite id="kronenberg-2022-eas-lpa-consensus" /><Cite id="nicholls-2025-muvalaplin-phase2" />

| Olpasiran (Amgen) | Same drug, first-event prevention | Same as above | OCEAN(a)-PreEvent, about 11,000 planned | October 2031 |
| Lepodisiran (Lilly) | siRNA, injection | 94% | ACCLAIM-Lp(a), 17,300 planned | March 2029 |
| Muvalaplin (Lilly) | Oral daily pill | 82–86% at 60–240 mg a day (intact Lp(a) assay) | MOVE-Lp(a), 10,450 planned | March 2031 |
| Zerlasiran (Silence) | siRNA, injection | 81–86% | None registered | No outcome trial yet |
| Pelacarsen (Novartis/Ionis) | Antisense, monthly injection | 72–80% at the top two doses | Lp(a)HORIZON, completed | July 2026 (actual); missed primary endpoint |

Who each trial enrolls matters if you want to know whether its result will apply to you:

- **OCEAN(a)-Outcomes:** adults 18 to 85 with a prior heart attack or coronary stent procedure and Lp(a) of at least 200 nmol/L. Its primary endpoint is coronary death, heart attack or urgent coronary revascularization, with no stroke component.<Cite id="ctgov-ocean-outcomes" />
- **OCEAN(a)-PreEvent:** adults 50 or older with no prior heart attack or stroke, multiple risk factors or evidence of atherosclerosis, and Lp(a) of at least 200 nmol/L.<Cite id="ctgov-ocean-preevent" />
- **ACCLAIM-Lp(a):** Lp(a) of at least 175 nmol/L, with either established disease or, from age 55, high risk without a prior event.<Cite id="ctgov-acclaim-lpa" />
- **MOVE-Lp(a):** Lp(a) of at least 175 nmol/L, with an event in the last 10 years or high risk, which can include a high coronary calcium score.<Cite id="ctgov-move-lpa" />

Registry dates are the sponsors' estimates and can move. For scale, HORIZON's registry entry lists primary completion on 16 July 2026, and the topline result came seven weeks later.<Cite id="ctgov-lpa-horizon" /><Cite id="novartis-2026-horizon-topline" /> Amgen also lists an olpasiran expanded access program, saying requests "may be considered" for adults with elevated Lp(a) and atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease.<Cite id="ctgov-olpasiran-expanded-access" /> Silence Therapeutics, as of March 2026, called zerlasiran a "Phase 3 ready program" that is "well positioned for a potential third-party partner to initiate Phase 3 development."<Cite id="silence-2026-fy2025-results" />

## What lowers Lp(a) today, and what does not

- **Dedicated Lp(a) drugs:** none approved.<Cite id="healio-2026-pelacarsen" />
- **PCSK9 inhibitors** (evolocumab, alirocumab): lower Lp(a) by roughly 15–30%, per the EAS; in the FOURIER trial, evolocumab's median reduction was 26.9%.<Cite id="kronenberg-2022-eas-lpa-consensus" /><Cite id="odonoghue-2019-fourier-lpa" /> They are prescribed to lower LDL cholesterol and are not registered for Lp(a) lowering.<Cite id="kronenberg-2022-eas-lpa-consensus" />
- **Statins:** may raise Lp(a) slightly; the evidence is mixed. A pooled analysis of six trials found average increases of 8.5% to 19.6% on statins, while a meta-analysis of 39 trials found no clinically important difference from placebo.<Cite id="tsimikas-2020-statins-lpa" /><Cite id="deboer-2022-statins-lpa-meta" /> The EAS says statin treatment should not be stopped on that account, because the benefit in people with high Lp(a) far outweighs any risk from a modest rise.<Cite id="kronenberg-2022-eas-lpa-consensus" /> If you have questions about yours, [this guide to statin side effects](/blog/statins-side-effects-when-to-stop) is a starting point for that conversation with your clinician.
- **Niacin:** not recommended for Lp(a) lowering, because two outcome trials showed no clinical benefit.<Cite id="kronenberg-2022-eas-lpa-consensus" />
- **Lifestyle:** minimal effect on Lp(a), which is more than 90% determined by genes.<Cite id="aha-2026-dyslipidemia-guideline-news" /><Cite id="kronenberg-2022-eas-lpa-consensus" /> Diet and exercise still lower LDL cholesterol, which is the lever guidelines point to; see [how to lower cholesterol without medication](/blog/how-to-lower-cholesterol-without-medication).
- **Lipoprotein apheresis:** a procedure that removes lipoproteins from the blood, lowering both Lp(a) and LDL cholesterol. Its FDA indication covers high-risk people with familial hypercholesterolemia and coronary or peripheral artery disease whose Lp(a) stays at 60 mg/dL or higher and LDL cholesterol at 100 mg/dL or higher despite maximally tolerated lipid-lowering therapy.<Cite id="koschinsky-2024-nla-lpa-update" />

## What to do with a high Lp(a) result now

1. **Know your number and its unit.** The EAS and NLA treat below 30 mg/dL (75 nmol/L) as low risk and 50 mg/dL (125 nmol/L) or higher as high risk, with an intermediate zone in between.<Cite id="kronenberg-2022-eas-lpa-consensus" /><Cite id="koschinsky-2024-nla-lpa-update" /> There is no exact conversion between the two units: the EAS advises against a standard factor because assays vary so much, and calls 2 to 2.5 only a "best guess."<Cite id="kronenberg-2022-eas-lpa-consensus" /> You can [convert your result between nmol/L and mg/dL](/tools/lpa-unit-converter) as a range. The unit matters for trials too: HORIZON used mg/dL, while the olpasiran, lepodisiran and muvalaplin trials set cut-offs of 175 or 200 nmol/L.
2. **One test is usually enough.** Levels stay relatively stable over a lifetime, so the 2026 guideline says repeat testing is generally not needed.<Cite id="aha-2026-dyslipidemia-guideline-news" /><Cite id="blumenthal-2026-dyslipidemia-guideline" />
3. **Work on what can change.** The NLA recommends early, more intensive risk-factor management for people with elevated Lp(a), primarily aimed at LDL cholesterol.<Cite id="koschinsky-2024-nla-lpa-update" /> The EAS adds that every other cardiovascular risk factor should be addressed: [LDL cholesterol and ApoB](/blog/apob-heart-disease-risk), blood pressure, smoking and diabetes.<Cite id="kronenberg-2022-eas-lpa-consensus" /> A [coronary calcium scan](/blog/cac-score-coronary-calcium-scan) shows whether calcified plaque has already formed; the ACC's summary of the 2026 guideline says it is recommended selectively, to refine risk when the usual estimate is uncertain.<Cite id="acc-2026-dyslipidemia-top-10" />
4. **Tell your first-degree relatives.** Because the level is inherited, the NLA recommends cascade screening of parents, siblings and children of people with elevated Lp(a).<Cite id="koschinsky-2024-nla-lpa-update" />
5. **Bring the trial question to your clinician.** Whether a trial or expanded access makes sense depends on your history, your number and your other treatment. The registry pages above list the eligibility criteria.
6. **Watch for 7 November.** The full HORIZON data are due then. Because the 90 mg/dL group was one of the two primary analyses in the registry, its result should be part of them, along with the size of the Lp(a) reduction.<Cite id="aha-2026-late-breaking" /><Cite id="ctgov-lpa-horizon" />
