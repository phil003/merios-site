## Is HbA1c 5.6% Normal?

**Yes — 5.6% is normal, but it's the last stop before prediabetes.** The American Diabetes Association defines normal as below 5.7%, prediabetes as 5.7–6.4%, and diabetes at 6.5% or higher.<Cite id="ada-2025-diagnosis" /> At 5.6% you're inside the normal band by a single tenth of a point. That's reassuring, but it also means there's no buffer — which is exactly why this value is worth understanding rather than ignoring.

## Where Does 5.6% Fall?

| Category | HbA1c (%) |
|----------|-----------|
| Optimal (longevity target) | 5.0–5.4 |
| **Your value (5.6%)** | **Top of normal** |
| Normal | &lt;5.7 |
| Prediabetes | 5.7–6.4 |
| Diabetes | &gt;=6.5 |

## What 5.6% Means for Your Average Blood Sugar

HbA1c estimates your average glucose over roughly the past 2–3 months, within the ~120-day life of a red blood cell.<Cite id="ada-2025-diagnosis" /> Using eAG = 28.7 × A1C − 46.7, a 5.6% A1C maps to an estimated average glucose of about **114 mg/dL** — a healthy 24-hour average.<Cite id="nathan-2008-adag" /> The caveat: the average can look fine while sharp post-meal spikes still occur, so two people at 5.6% can have very different glucose stability. ## Why "Top of Normal" Deserves Attention

A1C tends to creep, not jump. Risk climbs within the normal range: in a large US cohort, adults at 5.5–5.9% had nearly twice the diabetes risk, and a modestly higher heart disease risk, than those at 5.0–5.4%.<Cite id="selvin-2010-a1c-aric" /> Catching the drift here — while you're still technically normal — is the difference between a quick course-correction and years of elevated glucose. There's nothing to treat at 5.6%; there's plenty to maintain.

## How to Keep 5.6% From Climbing

- **Keep refined carbs and sugary drinks occasional, not daily.** Each daily serving of a sugary drink was linked to a higher risk of type 2 diabetes in pooled cohort studies.<Cite id="imamura-2015-ssb-diabetes" />
- **Stay active and keep muscle on.** Muscle is your largest glucose sink; resistance training improves insulin sensitivity directly.<Cite id="holten-2004-strength-training" />
- **Don't skimp on sleep.** Even short-term sleep loss nudges glucose and insulin upward.<Cite id="buxton-2010-sleep-restriction" />
- **Add context with fasting insulin.** A normal A1C with high [fasting insulin](/blog/fasting-insulin-levels-chart) means your body is working hard to keep glucose normal — an early signal A1C alone won't show.<Cite id="defronzo-2009-banting" /> Running both numbers through the [HOMA-IR calculator](/tools/homa-ir-calculator) puts a figure on that effort.

## When to Retest

For a normal-but-high A1C, rechecking in **6–12 months** is reasonable. The value of the second test is the trend: holding at 5.6% is fine; climbing toward 5.8% is a nudge to tighten habits.

## Watch the Direction

A single 5.6% is normal. Three readings drifting from 5.2% to 5.6% are a trajectory.
