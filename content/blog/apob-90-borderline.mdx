## Is ApoB 90 mg/dL Optimal or Risky?

**ApoB 90 mg/dL is borderline — not optimal, not yet high.** You're sitting right at the National Lipid Association's treatment threshold for people at borderline-to-intermediate risk (an ApoB of 90 mg/dL corresponds to an LDL-C of about 100 mg/dL) and above the European goal of &lt;80 mg/dL for high-risk people.<Cite id="nla-2024-apob" /><Cite id="esc-eas-2019-dyslipidaemia" /> It's a signal that lifestyle adjustments (and possibly lipid-lowering therapy down the road) deserve attention now, while the trajectory is still cheap to change.

## Where Does 90 mg/dL Fall?

| Category | ApoB goal or threshold (mg/dL) |
|----------|--------------------|
| Very high risk (NLA threshold / ESC/EAS goal) | &lt;60 / &lt;65 |
| High risk (NLA threshold / ESC/EAS goal) | &lt;70 / &lt;80 |
| Borderline-to-intermediate risk (NLA threshold) | &lt;90 |
| **Your value (90 mg/dL)** | **Borderline** |
| Moderate risk (ESC/EAS goal) | &lt;100 |

These figures come from the National Lipid Association's 2024 expert consensus and the 2019 ESC/EAS guidelines.<Cite id="nla-2024-apob" /><Cite id="esc-eas-2019-dyslipidaemia" /> For people with established cardiovascular disease and other very-high-risk profiles, the bar drops to **&lt;60–65 mg/dL**.

## Why ApoB Beats LDL Cholesterol

Most clinicians still focus on LDL-C, but **ApoB counts the number of atherogenic particles** — and the *number* of particles is what causes plaque, not the cholesterol they carry.<Cite id="sniderman-2019-apob" /><Cite id="marston-2022-apob-mi" />

Two patients can have identical LDL-C of 100 mg/dL with very different ApoB counts. The one with higher ApoB (more, smaller particles) carries meaningfully higher cardiovascular risk despite the same cholesterol number.<Cite id="nla-2024-apob" /> The 2019 ESC/EAS guidelines set ApoB goals alongside LDL-C goals, and the 2026 ACC/AHA guideline uses ApoB to check for leftover risk once LDL-C goals are met.<Cite id="esc-eas-2019-dyslipidaemia" /><Cite id="aha-acc-2026-dyslipidemia" />

## How to Lower ApoB from 90 to Under 80

ApoB responds to lifestyle, though each change moves it only modestly. The biggest levers, ranked by evidence:

1. **Reduce saturated fat to &lt;7% of calories** — the first dietary step in US guidelines. Swap butter, fatty meat, full-fat dairy for olive oil, fish, nuts; swapping saturated for unsaturated fat lowers both LDL-C and ApoB.<Cite id="ncep-atp3-2001" /><Cite id="koutsos-2024-rissci" />
2. **Add 10–25 g/day soluble fiber** — oats, beans, lentils, psyllium, chia. Soluble fiber binds bile acids, forcing the liver to pull cholesterol from circulation; in trials, about 10 g/day of psyllium lowered ApoB by roughly 5 mg/dL.<Cite id="ncep-atp3-2001" /><Cite id="gunness-2010-fibre-mechanisms" /><Cite id="jovanovski-2018-psyllium" />
3. **3–4 sessions/week zone-2 cardio** + 2 strength sessions — improves lipid metabolism and insulin sensitivity.<Cite id="piercy-2018-physical-activity" /><Cite id="kelley-2009-resistance-training" />
4. **Drop excess body fat** — even 5–10% body weight loss improves your lipid profile.<Cite id="jensen-2013-obesity-guideline" />
5. **Omega-3 fatty acids (EPA/DHA, 2–4 g/day)** — [lowers triglycerides](/blog/how-to-lower-triglycerides); at prescription doses of 4 g/day, ApoB fell only modestly.<Cite id="skulas-ray-2019-omega3" />

If after 12 weeks of consistent lifestyle change ApoB is still ≥80, talk to your physician about a low-dose statin or bempedoic acid. For people with established CVD or very high Lp(a), starting medication earlier is usually the right call.<Cite id="aha-acc-2026-dyslipidemia" />

## What to Recheck and When

- **Recheck ApoB at 12 weeks** after starting changes
- **Also check Lp(a) once in your life** (genetic; doesn't change with lifestyle)<Cite id="aha-acc-2026-dyslipidemia" /><Cite id="eas-2022-lpa-consensus" />
- **Track hs-CRP** alongside — [chronic inflammation](/blog/crp-level-above-3-heart-risk) adds to cardiovascular risk<Cite id="pearson-2003-crp" />

If your 12-week recheck shows ApoB has dropped below 80, you've reached even the European goal for high-risk people. If still 85+, that's the signal to escalate — either intensify lifestyle or add medication.
