Where a figure comes from trade press rather than a vendor page, it says so.

## The 30-second answer

- **Choose WHOOP Advanced Labs** if you already wear a WHOOP. Your labs land in the same app as your recovery, sleep and strain history, and nothing else on this page can do that. The multi-test tiers also get cheap per draw.
- **Choose Function Health** if you want the widest single panel, clinician review stated as non-optional, and no hardware in your life. It measures more in one sitting than WHOOP does, for six dollars more a year.

What changed in 2026 is that WHOOP dropped the membership gate. That makes Advanced Labs buyable by anyone — and a weaker buy for anyone who does not wear the strap, because the integration is the product.

## How we picked

These are the criteria, stated before the verdict, in the order we weighted them:

1. **Total first-year cost**, including any subscription the product requires or strongly implies, verified against the vendor's own pages.
2. **Biomarkers per draw**, not per year. Annual totals hide whether you get breadth in one sitting or the same panel repeated.
3. **Price per biomarker**, computed by us. Neither company publishes it.
4. **What each one measures**, and whether the catalogue number is one you can actually buy.
5. **Who reviews your results** — a clinician, an algorithm, or a report template.
6. **Draw logistics**, including where you can and cannot get drawn.
7. **Data export**, because both hold years of your history.

We did not accept either company's description of the other, and we treated August 2026 press coverage as secondary to the vendors' own live pages wherever the two could be checked against each other.

## What each one actually measures

WHOOP advertises **122+ distinct biomarkers**, and that number is not buyable as a single product — it is the union of every panel in the catalogue. The Comprehensive Health Panel, the one in the subscription, is 75. The five specialized panels (Heart 81, Performance 90, Women's 81, Metabolic 78, Men's 77) are $299 each on top and overlap heavily with the comprehensive panel rather than adding cleanly to it. Function, for its part, publishes no reconciliation of what counts as a "lab test."

At the category level both cover the same core: complete blood count, comprehensive metabolic panel, lipids, thyroid, sex hormones, inflammatory markers, iron studies and the common vitamins. Neither is missing anything a general physician would order; the differences are at the edges and in the add-ons.

## The Galleri addition, and what it does and does not establish

On **18 August 2026**, WHOOP added GRAIL's **Galleri** multi-cancer early detection test to Advanced Labs, at **$799**. It is the most consequential thing either company sells and the easiest to oversell, so it is worth being precise.

**On price:** WHOOP lists Galleri at **$799**, HSA/FSA eligible, ordered by a licensed physician with no separate visit, drawn at Quest, results in the WHOOP app in about two weeks. GRAIL's own consumer price is **$949**, so WHOOP's is a genuine discount to buying direct. Function Health also offers Galleri as a member add-on but **does not publish the price on its pricing page or FAQ, and we could not verify it.** We will not guess.

**What it is,** in GRAIL's own words on [its patient FAQ](https://www.galleri.com/patient/faqs): the test "has not been cleared or approved by the Food and Drug Administration," it was "developed — and its performance characteristics were determined — by GRAIL," and it "is intended to be used in addition to — and not replace — other cancer screening tests." GRAIL reports a **0.4% false positive rate**, a **positive predictive value of 62%**, and that nearly 99% of people aged 50 to 79 receive a "No Cancer Signal Detected" result.

**What the evidence shows:** the **NHS-Galleri trial**, the largest randomized test of the technology, enrolled **142,942 asymptomatic adults aged 50 to 77** and reported full results at ASCO in 2026. It **missed its primary endpoint** — three annual rounds added to standard screening produced no statistically significant reduction in combined stage III and stage IV diagnoses for the twelve prespecified cancers (hazard ratio 1.03, 95% CI 0.92 to 1.14). Secondary analyses were more favourable, with a reported 14% reduction in stage IV diagnoses and a 16% increase in stage I and II detection, and GRAIL has filed a premarket approval application with the FDA. Those secondary findings are hypothesis-generating, not proof.

The [American Cancer Society's position](https://www.cancer.org/cancer/screening/multi-cancer-early-detection-tests.html) is that "there are no clinical practice guidelines or official recommendations for using MCDs in the United States," and that these tests "do not replace current screening tests" such as mammography, cervical screening, colonoscopy, PSA testing and low-dose CT.

**The practical takeaway:** a negative Galleri result does not mean you do not have cancer, and is not a reason to skip a colonoscopy or a mammogram. A positive result starts a diagnostic workup with roughly a 62% chance of finding cancer and a 38% chance of a false alarm. It is a legitimate product with real published data behind it, sold into a category with no guideline support — and it should not decide this comparison either way.

If you intend to track a marker across a decade and across providers, that constrains both.

## The real trade-off

**WHOOP's case** is that a blood marker means something different when you can see what your body was doing around the draw. A cortisol reading next to three months of sleep data, or a lipid panel next to your actual training load, is more interpretable than the same number in isolation. That is a real argument, and it is the argument Merios makes too, which is why we will not pretend it is weak. Our explainers on [heart rate variability](/blog/heart-rate-variability-explained), [HRV by age](/blog/apple-watch-hrv-by-age) and [deep sleep](/blog/deep-sleep-how-to-increase) cover why continuous data adds signal.

**The cost of that case is the ecosystem.** The integration works only inside the WHOOP app, only with WHOOP hardware, and only while you keep paying — WHOOP devices stop functioning when the membership lapses. Switch to an Oura ring or an Apple Watch in two years and the labs stay in an app you no longer open. WHOOP's own metrics are not beyond question either: consumer [VO2 max estimates](/blog/apple-watch-vo2-max-accuracy) vary more than the marketing suggests, whichever brand you wear.

**Function's case is the opposite.** Lab-only, platform-neutral, broader in a single draw, indifferent to what you wear — and unable to tell you anything about your recovery, because it never sees it.

## Choose WHOOP Advanced Labs if

- You already wear a WHOOP and pay the membership. The integration is the product, and you have paid for half of it.
- You want frequent repeat testing. At four or six tests a year nothing here competes on cost per draw.
- You want the cheapest serious entry point: the $69 Basic Health Panel at 53 markers.
- You want Galleri at $799 rather than $949 direct, having read the section above.
- You are not in Arizona, Hawaii, Wyoming, North Dakota or South Dakota.

## Choose Function Health if

- You want the most distinct markers in a single draw. 100+ at the annual visit beats 75.
- You do not want a wearable, or you wear something other than a WHOOP and intend to keep it.
- You want imaging in the same catalogue. Function sells MRI, CT and brain testing as add-ons; WHOOP does not.
- You want one annual price with no hardware dependency and no device that stops working when you stop paying.

## When Merios is the wrong choice

We would rather you buy the right thing than the thing we sell. These are real cases where WHOOP Advanced Labs or Function Health is the correct purchase and Merios is not:

- **You do not currently get bloodwork and do not want to arrange it.** Merios has no lab and cannot order a draw. Buy WHOOP's $69 Basic Panel or a Function membership.
- **You want a clinician to look at your results.** Merios has none, is not a medical service, and cannot escalate a dangerous value to a human. Both alternatives include clinician review.
- **You wear a WHOOP and want the tightest integration.** WHOOP's own labs land inside the app you already use, with no upload step. Merios can ingest WHOOP data, but the first-party path is smoother.
- **You want cancer screening or imaging.** Merios does not do Galleri, MRI or CT. Both alternatives do.
- **You want maximum breadth in one draw.** Merios adds nothing to a panel you never ordered. Function's 100+ test annual visit is the widest single draw here.
- **You are not in the Apple ecosystem.** Much of the Merios value is Apple Health integration. Without an iPhone, much of the product does not apply.

## The bottom line

On verified 4 September 2026 prices, WHOOP Advanced Labs at $349 for two comprehensive draws and Function Health at $365 for two broader draws are close enough that price does not decide this. Price per biomarker — $2.33 against $2.28 — decides it even less.

What decides it is whether you wear a WHOOP. If you do, the integration is worth real money and nothing else here offers it. If you do not, dropping the membership gate has made Advanced Labs available to you and simultaneously less interesting: what remains is a competent 75-marker Quest panel with a clinician report, against a product measuring a third more in one sitting for six dollars more.

Buy on what you can verify: distinct markers per draw, whether you will still wear the same device in three years, and whether you can get your values back out of the app you put them into.
