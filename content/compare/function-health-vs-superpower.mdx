## The 30-second answer

- **Choose Function Health** if you want the deepest included panel, clinician review of every result as a stated part of the membership, and the option to add imaging. It is the more conservative, more medical choice.
- **Choose Superpower** if you want the lower entry price, a published results turnaround, and a more software-forward experience — and if you are comfortable that its marketed biomarker count is currently the subject of a false-advertising lawsuit it has not yet answered publicly.

There is no single winner. The two products are close enough on price that the deciding factors are what the marker count actually means, who reads your results, and how you want blood drawn.

## How we picked

These are the criteria, stated before the verdict, in the order we weighted them:

1. **Annual price**, verified against the vendor's own pricing page on the date of publication, not against secondary reviews.
2. **Price per biomarker**, computed by us from the marketed count and the verified price. Nobody in this category publishes this number, and it is the fastest way to see whether a marker count is doing marketing work.
3. **Marker overlap** — how much of each panel is the same core chemistry, and where the panels genuinely diverge.
4. **Who reviews your results** — a physician, a nurse practitioner, a dietitian, a coach, or an algorithm. These are very different products sold with similar language.
5. **Draw logistics** — how many draws per year, where, and whether at-home collection is available.
6. **Data export and portability** — whether you can get your results out in a usable form.
7. **Cancellation and refund terms**, because both of these are annual prepayments.

We did not accept either company's comparison of the other. Both publish pages comparing themselves to their rival; both are marketing.

## Price per biomarker

This is our own computation, not a vendor figure. It divides the verified annual price by the marketed marker count.

| Product | Marketed count | Annual price | Price per marketed unit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Function Health** | 160+ lab tests annually, across two draws | $365 | **$2.28** |
| **Superpower** | 150+ biomarkers annually, across baseline plus retest | $349 | **$2.33** |
| **Superpower** at promotional price | 150+ | $299 | **$1.99** |
| **Merios** | Not applicable — no lab draw included | $180 | Not comparable |

On the marketed numbers, these two products are within five cents per unit of each other. That is the finding: **price per biomarker does not separate them.** Anyone telling you one is dramatically better value on price alone is comparing a promotional rate against a standing rate.

The number moves once you stop taking the marketed counts at face value, and there are two independent reasons to do that.

**First, the units are not the same thing.** Function counts "lab tests." Superpower counts "biomarkers." Those words are used interchangeably in marketing and are not interchangeable in a lab: a single test can report several values, and a panel count can include ratios calculated from values already counted once. Comparing 160 of one to 150 of the other is not like for like, and neither company publishes a reconciliation.

**Second, both counts are annual totals across two draws.** Superpower's FAQ describes the retest as covering **60+ biomarkers** of the 150+ annual figure, implying a baseline panel of roughly 90. Many people never return for the second draw. Take the baseline and skip the retest and your real cost is nearer **$349 divided by about 90, or $3.88 per biomarker** — 66 percent above the headline. Function does not publish a first-draw and second-draw split, so we cannot compute its equivalent and will not guess one.

## The lawsuit, and what it does and does not establish

On **26 January 2026**, Function Health filed suit against Superpower in the U.S. District Court for the Central District of California. The case is **Function Health, Inc. v. Superpower Health Inc. et al, No. 2:26-cv-00810**, and the cause of action is false advertising under the Lanham Act, 15 U.S.C. section 1125. The docket is public on [Justia](https://dockets.justia.com/docket/california/cacdce/2:2026cv00810/1004005).

According to the complaint as summarized by the law firm ArentFox Schiff in [its analysis of the case](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/longevity-lens/competing-biomarker-providers-clash-function-health-sues-superpower), Function alleges that:

- Superpower's marketed "100+ lab tests" and "100+ biomarkers" reflected approximately **55 direct measurements**, with the remainder derived from calculated metrics rather than new biological signals.
- Superpower's "real clinical team" available "24/7" was in practice responses **within 24 hours on weekdays**, from dietitians and health coaches rather than physicians or nurses.
- Superpower's claim of "3,000+" lab locations overstated the reality, given that Quest Diagnostics operates roughly 2,250 U.S. patient service centers.

**Every one of those is an allegation by a direct competitor in a pending case.** None has been proven. Superpower's response deadline was extended by stipulation into late April 2026, and as of the most recent docket activity we could verify, no court had ruled on the merits, and no injunction had issued. Superpower did not provide a statement to the trade press covering the filing. A company being sued is not a company being found liable, and Function is not a neutral party — it is the plaintiff, and it competes for the same customer.

What you can verify yourself, independently of the lawsuit, is narrower and more useful: **Superpower's own biomarker directory labels a number of entries "Derived Biomarker."** Those include the LDL/HDL ratio, the Atherogenic Index of Plasma, the Castelli Risk Indexes, and the Neutrophil-to-Lymphocyte Ratio, each carrying Superpower's disclaimer that the ratio or index "is not an FDA-cleared test." That is not a scandal — derived ratios are clinically standard and useful, and our own [reference-range coverage](/blog/optimal-vs-normal-blood-test-ranges) treats them as meaningful. But a derived ratio is arithmetic on numbers you already paid for. It is not an additional thing drawn from your arm, and counting it as one inflates a marker count without adding information.

The practical takeaway for a buyer: **do not choose between these two on marker count.** It is the least reliable number either company publishes, and it is now contested in federal court.

Function states clinician review of every result as an included, non-optional part of the membership. Superpower describes a care team of licensed providers alongside its AI layer but does not specify which professional type reviews an individual result — the distinction the lawsuit contests. Both companies take an annual prepayment and neither refunds it. That is the most financially consequential line in this comparison, and it is buried in both FAQs.

On portability: Function's PDF export is fine for handing to a physician but poor for tracking a marker across years, since you cannot easily get the values into a spreadsheet. Superpower's language is broader but unspecific. Neither publishes a documented personal-data API. If trend tracking matters, read [how often you should actually get bloodwork](/blog/how-often-blood-work) before committing — testing cadence is a bigger lever on cost than either sticker price.

## Marker overlap

We compared the two published biomarker directories at the category level, not line by line, so we will not claim a precise overlap percentage we did not compute.

At that level the panels overlap heavily. Both cover the standard core: complete blood count, comprehensive metabolic panel, full lipids including ApoB and Lp(a), thyroid, sex hormones, inflammatory markers, iron studies, and vitamin D and B12. For most buyers, the markers you will actually act on are in both. The divergence is at the edges — specialty add-ons, heavy metals, and biological age scoring, where both use proprietary models rather than a published algorithm. If biological age is your reason for buying, understand [how biological age is calculated from blood](/blog/biological-age-calculator-blood-test) first, because proprietary scores are not comparable across products.

## Choose Function Health if

- You want the panel depth and you want a clinician's review of every result stated as part of what you bought, not implied.
- You want imaging under the same membership. Function advertises adding MRI and CT anytime, plus the Galleri multi-cancer test and brain testing, priced on top of the $365.
- You value the more explicit, more conservative claims. In a category where marketing language is being litigated, that is worth something.
- You want the widest single catalogue — blood, imaging and specialty add-ons — bought from one vendor rather than assembled.
- You can absorb $365 up front knowing it is non-refundable.

## Choose Superpower if

- You want the lower entry price, especially if you can catch the promotional first year — a $66 saving against Function's standing rate in year one, and $16 a year after that.
- You want a published turnaround. Superpower commits to results in "10 days or less"; Function publishes no equivalent guarantee.
- You will never use imaging. Function's add-ons are priced on top of the $365, so if you only want blood, you are paying into a catalogue you will not touch.
- You prefer a heavier software and AI layer, and you are comfortable weighing the marker-count dispute yourself rather than accepting Function's account of it.

## Where Merios fits

Merios is analysis software for blood tests you already have: it parses lab PDFs from any lab, tracks markers over time, and combines them with Apple Health data. It costs less than either product here because it does not include a blood draw — which is most of what you are paying Function and Superpower for.

It is a different category, not a cheaper version of these two. If you already get comprehensive bloodwork, it is worth a look, and we cover the wider category in our roundup of [blood test tracking apps](/blog/best-blood-test-tracking-apps). If you do not, it solves nothing on its own.

## When Merios is the wrong choice

We would rather you buy the right thing than the thing we sell. These are real cases where Function Health or Superpower is the correct purchase and Merios is not:

- **You do not currently get bloodwork and do not want to arrange it.** Merios has no lab. You would need to source a comprehensive panel yourself, and buying one a la carte typically costs more than either membership. Buy Function or Superpower.
- **You want a clinician to look at your results.** Merios has none. It cannot flag a dangerous value to a human, and it is not a medical service. If a physician reviewing your labs is what you actually want, Function states this explicitly as part of the membership. Buy Function.
- **You want imaging.** Merios does not do MRI, CT, or cancer screening. Function does.
- **You want one price that covers everything.** Merios' lower sticker price excludes the lab fee. Once you add a comprehensive panel, your total annual cost can exceed $365, at which point Function is both cheaper and more complete.
- **You need at-home blood collection.** Merios cannot arrange a draw of any kind. Both alternatives can — Function advertises mobile phlebotomy, and Superpower offers at-home collection for an additional fee.
- **You are not in the Apple ecosystem.** A significant share of the Merios value is Apple Health integration. Without an iPhone, much of the product does not apply, and both alternatives here are platform-neutral.

## The bottom line

On verified September 2026 prices, Function Health at $365 and Superpower at $349 are close enough that price is not the deciding factor, and price per biomarker — $2.28 against $2.33 — separates them by almost nothing. What separates them is the reliability of what those numbers describe.

Function makes narrower, more specific claims about who reads your results, and publishes a price history you can check. Superpower is cheaper at the entry point, publishes a results turnaround Function does not, and is defending a false-advertising suit over the exact claims a buyer here most wants to trust. That suit is unresolved and may end with Superpower fully vindicated. Until it does, buy on what you can verify yourself: who reviews your results, where you get drawn, what you can export, and what you cannot get refunded.
