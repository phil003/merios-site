## Is Ferritin 30 ng/mL Bad?

**Technically normal, functionally borderline.** 30 ng/mL falls within typical lab "normal" ranges (MedlinePlus lists 13–150 for women and 30–400 for men), but those lower limits come from reference populations that often weren't screened for iron deficiency, and a ferritin below 30 is the more accurate line for diagnosing it.<Cite id="medlineplus-ferritin" /><Cite id="truong-2024-ferritin-reference" /> Iron deficiency without anemia can cause fatigue, restless legs, exercise intolerance, and difficulty concentrating — even when hemoglobin is "normal."<Cite id="auerbach-2025-iron-deficiency" />

## Where Does 30 ng/mL Fall?

| Category | Ferritin (ng/mL) |
|----------|------------------|
| Iron deficient (absent stores) | &lt;15 |
| Low stores (below the common 30 cut-off) | 15–30 |
| **Your value (30 ng/mL)** | **Borderline — at the common cut-off** |
| Low normal | 30–50 |
| Sufficient | 50–70 |
| Often called "optimal" (no guideline target) | 70–100 |
| High (investigate) | &gt;150 (women), &gt;200 (men) |

The lab "normal" range is wide because its lower limit was drawn from reference populations that often included iron-deficient people, not from evidence about function.<Cite id="truong-2024-ferritin-reference" /> The deficiency and overload lines in the table come from WHO and UK gastroenterology guidelines, and the AGA uses **45 ng/mL** as its cut-off when anemia is present.<Cite id="who-2020-ferritin" /><Cite id="bsg-2021-ida" /><Cite id="aga-2020-ida" /> No guideline sets an "optimal" ferritin; in trials, iron eased fatigue in non-anemic women whose ferritin was below **50 ng/mL**.<Cite id="vaucher-2012-fatigue-rct" /><Cite id="verdon-2003-fatigue-rct" /> If your result is even lower, see what [a ferritin of 15](/blog/ferritin-level-15) signals about early iron deficiency.

## Symptoms of Functionally Low Ferritin

If your ferritin is 30 and you're experiencing any of these, low iron may be contributing even though hemoglobin is "normal":

- Persistent fatigue not explained by sleep
- Hair shedding (often diffuse, not patchy)
- Restless legs at night
- Cold hands and feet
- Exercise intolerance / breathlessness on stairs
- Brittle nails (spoon-shaped in advanced deficiency)
- Brain fog / poor concentration
- Pica (cravings for ice, dirt, starch)

Fatigue, restless legs, pica, poor concentration and exercise intolerance are the symptoms most tied to iron deficiency itself; brittle nails, hair loss and cold hands and feet are listed mainly once anemia develops, and the evidence on hair loss is mixed.<Cite id="auerbach-2025-iron-deficiency" /><Cite id="medlineplus-ida" /><Cite id="nhlbi-ida" /><Cite id="trost-2006-hair-loss" />

## Why Ferritin Drops Before Hemoglobin

Your body prioritizes oxygen-carrying capacity. When iron intake drops, hemoglobin holds steady (you still need to breathe) but ferritin (storage) drops first. By the time hemoglobin actually falls, you've usually been iron-depleted for some time.<Cite id="who-2020-ferritin" /><Cite id="auerbach-2025-iron-deficiency" />

This is why **ferritin is the early warning** and hemoglobin is the late warning. If you wait for "anemia" on a CBC, you've ignored your body's first signal.

The red cell indices often shift before hemoglobin does, which is why a falling MCV or MCH can corroborate a borderline ferritin;<Cite id="bsg-2021-ida" /> our guide to [what low MCV and MCH mean on a blood test](/blog/mcv-mch-blood-test-meaning) shows which patterns point back toward iron.

## How to Raise Ferritin from 30 to 70+

Iron supplementation works but it's slow. Practical protocol:

1. **Iron supplement: one tablet, about 65 mg elemental iron** (ferrous sulfate is the guideline standard; ferrous bisglycinate caused fewer GI side effects in trials, mostly in pregnancy)<Cite id="bsg-2021-ida" /><Cite id="fischer-2023-bisglycinate" />
2. **Vitamin C is optional** (a 440-patient trial found that adding it to iron tablets didn't improve hemoglobin or ferritin recovery)<Cite id="li-2020-vitamin-c" />
3. **On an empty stomach if you can** (taking iron with meals can cut absorption by up to 75%)<Cite id="bsg-2021-ida" />
4. **Avoid coffee, tea, dairy within 2 hours** (calcium and tannins block iron absorption, and coffee still cut absorption when drunk an hour after a meal)<Cite id="hallberg-1991-calcium" /><Cite id="hurrell-1999-polyphenols" /><Cite id="morck-1983-coffee" />
5. **Take every other day** rather than daily — alternate-day dosing absorbs a larger share of each dose than daily dosing (hepcidin signaling) and causes fewer GI side effects, though at the same total dose ferritin ends up similar<Cite id="stoffel-2017-alternate-day" /><Cite id="von-siebenthal-2023-alternate-day" />
6. **Recheck ferritin at 12 weeks** — adjust dose

If iron supplements aren't working after 12 weeks, see [Iron Supplements Not Working? Here's Why](/blog/iron-supplements-not-working).

## Why Iron Supplementation Sometimes Fails

If you've been supplementing and ferritin won't budge, common causes:

- **Poor absorption** — H. pylori infection, low stomach acid, celiac, IBD<Cite id="bsg-2021-ida" />
- **Wrong form** — polysaccharide iron raised hemoglobin less than ferrous sulfate in a trial in children, and slow-release tablets release iron past the main absorption site<Cite id="powers-2017-polysaccharide-iron" /><Cite id="bsg-2021-ida" />
- **Blockers** — coffee, tea, calcium taken with iron<Cite id="hurrell-1999-polyphenols" /><Cite id="hallberg-1991-calcium" />
- **Inflammation** — high CRP suppresses iron utilization (functional iron deficiency)<Cite id="weiss-2019-anemia-inflammation" />
- **Hidden blood loss** — heavy menstrual periods, GI bleeding, frequent blood donation<Cite id="bsg-2021-ida" />
- **Underlying disease** — celiac, IBD, autoimmune conditions<Cite id="bsg-2021-ida" /><Cite id="weiss-2019-anemia-inflammation" />

If basic supplementation doesn't work, push your physician for a workup beyond [a basic CBC](/blog/cbc-blood-test-results-explained).
