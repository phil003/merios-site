## The 30-second answer

- **Choose Mito Health** if you want one comprehensive draw without an annual bundle, a live consult you can book, or add-ons bought one at a time. Its Essential panel is the cheapest broad draw on this page. Check the tier before you buy: Lp(a) is not in Essential, and particle testing is only in Pro.
- **Choose Function Health** if you want the most cardiovascular coverage included by default and two draws a year for one price. ApoB, Lp(a) and LDL particle testing are all inside the $365 membership.
- **Choose Superpower** if you want the lowest bundled price: $199 for one draw that includes ApoB, Lp(a), hs-CRP and homocysteine, plus a complimentary follow-up test for new members.

For cholesterol specifically: Function includes the most without choosing anything, Superpower includes ApoB and Lp(a) for the least money, and Mito makes you pick a panel tier to get them.

## How we picked

These are the criteria, stated before the verdict, in the order we weighted them:

1. **First-year cost, all-in**: membership, panel and draw fee, verified on each vendor's own pages.
2. **What is in the draw**, especially cholesterol and cardiovascular markers, because that is the question most people bring to this comparison.
3. **Price per biomarker**, computed by us. None of the three publishes it.
4. **What the marker counts count**: urinalysis entries, blood-smear descriptors and calculated ratios.
5. **Follow-up**: who reviews results, whether you can talk to someone, and how retesting works.
6. **Draw logistics, state availability and refunds.**

Some searchers also ask about Lifeforce. We did not re-verify its pricing or panels today, so it is not compared here.

Sources, verified 23 October 2026: the biomarker lists on Mito's [Essential](https://mitohealth.com/products/mito-essentials), [Core](https://mitohealth.com/products/mito-core) and [Pro](https://mitohealth.com/products/mito-pro) pages; Function's [what we test](https://www.functionhealth.com/what-we-test) page, which marks the markers tested twice a year; and Superpower's [biomarker directory](https://superpower.com/biomarkers). Superpower's heart section also lists oxidized LDL, ADMA and SDMA, plus many calculated ratios. Mito's Pro panel adds LDL particle pattern, small LDL particles and large HDL and VLDL particle counts.

**What the guidelines say about these markers.** The 2018 US cholesterol guideline from the AHA, ACC and partner societies lists ApoB of 130 mg/dL or higher, Lp(a) of 50 mg/dL (125 nmol/L) or higher and hs-CRP of 2.0 mg/L or higher as "risk-enhancing factors" that can favor statin therapy in adults at intermediate risk, "if measured in selected individuals" ([Grundy et al., Circulation, 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30586774/)). In other words, the US guideline treats them as optional additions, not routine tests for everyone. The European Atherosclerosis Society goes further on Lp(a) and recommends testing it at least once in adults ([Kronenberg et al., European Heart Journal, 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36036785/)). LDL particle number and size are not among the risk-enhancing factors the US guideline lists. Our explainers on [ApoB and heart disease risk](/blog/apob-heart-disease-risk) and on [what a high Lp(a) means](/blog/lp-a-lipoprotein-a-high) cover how to read those numbers.

The practical reading: if you have never had Lp(a) measured, Function, Superpower and Mito Core all include it, and Mito Essential does not. If you want LDL particle testing, the choice is Function or Mito Pro. What to do about any result is a conversation for your clinician, not for any of these dashboards.

### Calcium scans

All three sell a coronary artery calcium (CAC) scan on top of the blood panel:

- **Mito**: $249 for members, $348.60 for non-members, no draw fee, no referral needed, for ages 40 to 75 with at least one heart risk factor and no known heart disease. Mito says reports usually arrive within 24 hours ([Mito CAC scan page](https://mitohealth.com/products/coronary-artery-calcium-cac-scan)).
- **Function**: a gated Heart CT with a CAC score at $349, booked through Function or directly with its imaging partner Ezra ([functionhealth.com/scans](https://www.functionhealth.com/scans)).
- **Superpower**: a Heart Calcium Scan at $379, plus the $199 membership ([Superpower Heart Calcium Scan](https://superpower.com/marketplace/products/heart-calcium-scan)).

The same 2018 guideline suggests considering a calcium score in adults aged 40 to 75 at intermediate risk when the decision about a statin is uncertain, and describes how a score of zero, or of 100 or more, shifts that decision, with exceptions. The scan uses a low dose of radiation, which Mito's FAQ puts at about 1 to 2 millisieverts. Whether you should have one is a decision to make with your clinician.

## What the marker counts actually count

Mito's own comparison page warns that some services "count sub-components of a single test as separate biomarkers" and advises checking "the unique analyte count before comparing numbers." That is good advice, and it applies to every company here, Mito included.

- **Mito.** The Essential page lists 117 entries against a headline of 118; Mito says male and female versions differ by a marker or two. Of those entries, 25 are urinalysis items such as urine color, appearance and crystals, and 50 sit in a "Blood Health" group. That group includes the usual counts alongside names that come from a blood smear, such as acanthocytes, schistocytes, rouleaux and "parasite identification." A smear is a microscope look at the size, shape and number of blood cells, which [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/lab-tests/blood-smear/) describes as a common follow-up when a complete blood count is abnormal. Core and Pro carry 27 urinalysis entries each.
- **Function.** Its membership list includes 20 urinalysis entries, and it labels several entries, among them LDL cholesterol, non-HDL cholesterol and biological age, "calculation derived from lab tests." Its blood section lists 10 entries, one of them blood type, and six white-cell entries sit under immune regulation.
- **Superpower.** Its own comparison page against Mito describes its draw as "103 biomarkers (57 distinct and 46 calculated)" and says it carries no urinalysis. Its biomarker directory labels many cardiovascular entries "Derived Biomarker." Function sued Superpower in January 2026 alleging, as trade press reported, that the "100+ biomarkers" claim was misleading because part of the count is calculated rather than measured ([Fitt Insider, 10 February 2026](https://insider.fitt.co/function-sues-superpower-over-biomarker-claims/)). The [court docket](https://dockets.justia.com/docket/california/cacdce/2:2026cv00810/1004005) records that the parties told the court they had settled, and the case was dismissed on 12 June 2026 with no ruling on the merits.

None of the three headline numbers is a count of distinct blood tests, and each company counts differently. Compare the markers you care about, not the totals.

## Honest limits of each

**Mito Health.** The price has several moving parts: membership, panel, lab, state and draw fee. In New York and New Jersey the Core panel costs about 2.3 times its Quest price. The base panel leaves out Lp(a) and homocysteine. Its own pages disagree on how fast results arrive. Mito describes its consults as "health education, not medical advice" that do not create a patient-provider relationship. There is no native app. And Mito sells supplements, through Fullscript at member prices, in the same product that writes your action plan; Mito says its clinicians are salaried and earn nothing extra for recommending more.

**Function Health.** The $365 is paid up front and is not refundable. ApoB, Lp(a) and particle testing happen once a year, not at both draws. The mid-year draw does not roll over. It is not offered in Rhode Island, Hawaii or Puerto Rico, the New York and New Jersey price is not published, and neither is the at-home fee. No live consult is advertised on the pages we read.

**Superpower.** By its own account, 46 of its 103 markers are calculated, and its draw carries no urinalysis and, per its directory, no LDL particle testing. It is unavailable in nine states and does not refund. Its list price has also changed during 2026: it was $349 a year when we checked on 3 September and is $199 today, as our [Function Health vs Superpower comparison](/compare/function-health-vs-superpower) documents.

## Who should pick which

### Choose Mito Health if

- You want one comprehensive draw this year without an annual bundle. Essential at $113.60 to $203.60 all-in is the cheapest broad draw on this page.
- You already know which markers you want and would rather buy those than a fixed list.
- You want a live 30-minute consult with the care team, at $39 as a member.
- You want a calcium scan at the lowest price here, $249 for members, and you meet Mito's eligibility criteria.
- You do not live in Hawaii or Rhode Island, or you are happy to test in a neighboring state.

### Choose Function Health if

- You want ApoB, Lp(a) and LDL particle testing without choosing a tier.
- You will actually use both draws. Skip the mid-year visit and your real cost per test rises to $3.65.
- You want phone apps, one fixed price, and imaging available from the same membership.

### Choose Superpower if

- You want the lowest bundled price that still includes ApoB, Lp(a), hs-CRP and homocysteine.
- You are a new member and will use the complimentary follow-up test.
- You want your wearable connected: its pricing page lists Apple Health, WHOOP and Oura.
- You live in one of the 41 states, or DC, that it serves.

## When to pick none of them

- **Your doctor already orders an annual panel.** You would be paying again for a draw you already get. Ask whether ApoB or Lp(a) can be added to it; coverage depends on your plan. Then keep the results yourself, in the lab's portal, a spreadsheet, or one of the [blood test tracking apps we compared](/blog/best-blood-test-tracking-apps).
- **Your Lp(a) is already known.** The European recommendation is to test it at least once. You do not need a membership to learn the same number again; ask your clinician whether a repeat is useful.
- **You want a diagnosis or treatment.** All three say on their own sites that they complement a primary care physician rather than replace one.

## Where Merios fits

Merios is iPhone software that reads lab report PDFs and photos from any lab, tracks markers over time and puts them next to Apple Health data on sleep, HRV and activity. The lab report scan is part of Merios Plus, at $44 a year. It does not test and includes no blood draw, so it is not a cheaper version of anything on this page. Each of the three companies here already stores and trends its own results. Merios only adds something if you want results from several labs in one place next to your wearable data.

## When Merios is the wrong choice

- **You need blood drawn.** Merios has no lab and cannot order a test. Buy one of the three above.
- **You want a clinician to review your results.** Merios has none, is not a medical service, and cannot escalate a worrying value to a person.
- **You want a calcium scan or any imaging.** Merios offers neither.
- **You test with one company and like its dashboard.** Mito members can already upload past labs, and Function and Superpower have their own apps.
- **You do not use an iPhone.** Merios runs on iPhone only, on iOS 15.1 or later.

## The bottom line

On prices verified 23 October 2026, Mito Health is the cheapest way to get one broad draw, at $113.60 to $203.60 for its Essential panel. Superpower is the cheapest bundle that includes ApoB and Lp(a), at $199. Function Health is the only one that includes LDL particle testing and two draws for one price, at $365.

For cholesterol testing, that gives a short rule: for Lp(a), anything except Mito Essential; for particle testing, Function or Mito Pro. The marker counts each company advertises are counted differently and should not decide this. Buy on the specific markers you want, the state you live in, and whether you will use the second draw.
