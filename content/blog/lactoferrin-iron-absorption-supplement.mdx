## What Is Lactoferrin?

Lactoferrin is a glycoprotein found naturally in milk, colostrum (first milk after birth), tears, saliva, and neutrophils. It's present in high concentrations in early milk, and it is part of the body's innate defenses.<Cite id="rosa-2017-lactoferrin-review" />

In the body, lactoferrin serves multiple roles:
- **Iron transport:** Binds and carries iron with extremely high affinity (Kd ~10^-20 M)<Cite id="baker-2004-lactoferrin-iron-binding" />
- **Antimicrobial:** Sequesters iron, depriving pathogens of this essential nutrient<Cite id="baker-2004-lactoferrin-iron-binding" /><Cite id="rosa-2017-lactoferrin-review" />
- **Immune modulation:** Has anti-inflammatory effects, including lowering the inflammatory signal interleukin-6 (IL-6)<Cite id="rosa-2017-lactoferrin-review" />
- **Intestinal barrier:** May help protect the tight junctions of the gut lining, though this comes mainly from laboratory studies<Cite id="hirotani-2008-lactoferrin-barrier" />

Commercial lactoferrin is typically purified from bovine (cow) milk.<Cite id="dyrda-terniuk-2023-bovine-lactoferrin" /> It's well-tolerated and available without prescription.<Cite id="efsa-2012-bovine-lactoferrin" />

**Key insight:** Lactoferrin is not really an iron supplement: the doses used in trials supply only micrograms of iron a day. Its proposed benefit is on how the body handles iron — lowering the inflammatory signal IL-6 so that stored iron can be released into the blood.<Cite id="rosa-2017-lactoferrin-review" /><Cite id="paesano-2010-lactoferrin-trials" />

## How Lactoferrin Improves Iron Absorption

### The Iron Absorption Problem

Standard iron supplements (ferrous sulfate, ferrous gluconate) provide elemental iron, which must be taken up through a specific iron transporter (DMT1, divalent metal transporter 1) on the cells lining the duodenum.<Cite id="kumar-2022-ida-review" />

Problems with standard iron:
- **Competes for absorption** with other minerals, notably calcium<Cite id="hallberg-1991-calcium" />
- **Affected by pH and food components** (phytates, tannins, polyphenols bind iron and reduce absorption)<Cite id="kumar-2022-ida-review" />
- **Poor bioavailability** (in iron-depleted women, only about 16–22% of a ferrous sulfate dose was absorbed; the rest is lost in stool)<Cite id="stoffel-2017-alternate-day" />
- **GI side effects** (nausea, constipation, dark stools)<Cite id="tolkien-2015-ferrous-sulfate-gi" /><Cite id="bsg-2021-ida" />

### How Lactoferrin Circumvents These Issues

Several mechanisms have been proposed, but most come from cell studies, and the human data don't all support them:

1. **Transcytosis:** Lactoferrin enters intestinal epithelial cells via a specific lactoferrin receptor.<Cite id="jiang-2011-lactoferrin-receptor" /> It has been proposed that it then carries iron across the cell, but in cell studies very little intact lactoferrin made it through.<Cite id="sanchez-1996-caco2-iron-transport" /><Cite id="conesa-2009-caco2-iron-transport" />

2. **Iron protection:** Lactoferrin holds iron in the Fe3+ (ferric) state and keeps hold of it even at low pH, unlike its blood counterpart, transferrin.<Cite id="baker-2004-lactoferrin-iron-binding" />

3. **Reduced dietary interference:** In theory, iron held inside lactoferrin should be less exposed to dietary phytates, tannins, and polyphenols, but this hasn't been shown in people.

4. **Probiotic support:** Lactoferrin may act as a prebiotic, supporting beneficial gut bacteria.<Cite id="dyrda-terniuk-2023-bovine-lactoferrin" />

In people, the picture is different: iron bound to lactoferrin was absorbed about as well as iron from ferrous sulfate in young women,<Cite id="lonnerdal-2006-lactoferrin-absorption" /> adding iron-free lactoferrin to a meal raised absorption in infants,<Cite id="mikulic-2020-lactoferrin-infants" /> and a 2022 meta-analysis found lactoferrin actually lowered fractional iron absorption compared with ferrous sulfate while still improving hemoglobin and ferritin — pointing to its anti-inflammatory effect, rather than better absorption, as the likely mechanism.<Cite id="zhao-2022-lactoferrin-meta-analysis" />

## Evidence: Lactoferrin vs. Iron Supplements

### In Vitro Evidence

Laboratory studies in Caco-2 intestinal cells (a model of the human gut lining) give mixed results:
- A 1996 study found more iron crossed the cell layer from lactoferrin than from iron citrate.<Cite id="sanchez-1996-caco2-iron-transport" />
- A 2009 study found the reverse: iron crossed more readily from citrate than from (recombinant human) lactoferrin, and very little intact lactoferrin got through.<Cite id="conesa-2009-caco2-iron-transport" />

### Human Studies in Pregnant Women

Most clinical evidence comes from pregnancy, where iron requirements are high:

**A 2009 Italian double-blind RCT** (100 pregnant women with iron-deficiency anemia):
- Group A: Bovine lactoferrin 100 mg twice daily
- Group B: Ferrous sulfate 520 mg once daily
- **Result:** After 30 days, both groups improved hemoglobin, ferritin and serum iron, with no significant difference between them; abdominal pain and constipation were less common with lactoferrin.<Cite id="nappi-2009-lactoferrin-pregnancy-rct" />

**Italian trials published in 2010** (pregnant and non-pregnant women with iron deficiency or anemia):
- Group A: Bovine lactoferrin 100 mg twice daily
- Group B: Ferrous sulfate 520 mg daily
- **Result:** Lactoferrin raised hematological values and lowered IL-6, while ferrous sulfate did not raise them in these trials.<Cite id="paesano-2010-lactoferrin-trials" />

**A 2017 meta-analysis** (4 randomized trials, 600 pregnant women):
- Lactoferrin versus ferrous iron, each taken on its own
- **Result:** Hemoglobin rose slightly more with lactoferrin at 4 weeks (0.77 g/dL, a borderline result), with no difference in mild anemia, fewer GI side effects, and no difference in preterm birth or birthweight.<Cite id="abu-hashim-2017-lactoferrin-pregnancy-meta" />

In these trials lactoferrin was given instead of iron, not added to it. It matched or beat ferrous sulfate with fewer GI side effects, but the trials are small and their results vary widely,<Cite id="christofi-2024-lactoferrin-meta-analysis" /> and one of the larger trials (Rezk 2016, from Egypt) has since been retracted.<Cite id="rezk-2016-retraction-notice" />

### Human Studies in Non-Pregnant Populations

Evidence is more limited. In the 2010 Italian trials, lactoferrin also raised hematological values in non-pregnant women of child-bearing age,<Cite id="paesano-2010-lactoferrin-trials" /> and a small study of 16 female distance runners found that adding lactoferrin to iron kept ferritin from falling over 8 weeks of training, while it dropped with iron alone.<Cite id="koikawa-2008-lactoferrin-runners" /> Larger trials are needed in non-pregnant populations.

**Key insight:** Evidence is strongest in pregnant women with iron-deficiency anemia. For otherwise healthy people supplementing iron preventively, evidence is sparse and the mechanism is still debated.<Cite id="zhao-2022-lactoferrin-meta-analysis" />

## Lactoferrin + Iron + Vitamin C: The Stack

No trial has tested this exact three-part combination, so treat it as a hypothesis rather than a proven protocol:

| Component | Dose | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **Lactoferrin** | 200 mg daily (100 mg twice daily, as in trials) | Proposed: lowers IL-6 so stored iron can be released |
| **Elemental iron** | 50-100 mg | Provides iron (the standard oral dose range) |
| **Vitamin C** | 500 mg | Helps absorb iron from food; added no benefit to iron tablets in a large trial |

**Why this might work, and the caveats:** Lactoferrin trials used 100 mg twice daily on its own, and standard oral iron is 50–100 mg of elemental iron a day.<Cite id="nappi-2009-lactoferrin-pregnancy-rct" /><Cite id="bsg-2021-ida" /> Vitamin C enhances non-heme iron absorption from food, but adding it to iron tablets didn't improve hemoglobin or ferritin recovery in a 440-patient trial.<Cite id="ods-iron" /><Cite id="li-2020-vitamin-c" /> Whether stacking all three beats lactoferrin or iron alone is untested.

### Dosing Strategy

**For mild deficiency or prevention:**
- Lactoferrin 200 mg daily (the dose used in trials)<Cite id="nappi-2009-lactoferrin-pregnancy-rct" />
- Ferrous sulfate (or ferrous gluconate) 50 mg elemental iron daily
- Vitamin C 500 mg daily (optional; it didn't add benefit to iron tablets in a large trial)<Cite id="li-2020-vitamin-c" />
- Take on empty stomach or with small meal (empty stomach is better absorbed, since food can cut absorption by up to 75%, but take with food if GI upset occurs)<Cite id="bsg-2021-ida" />

**For moderate anemia:**
- Lactoferrin 200 mg daily (higher doses haven't shown additional benefit)
- Ferrous sulfate 65 mg elemental iron daily (one standard tablet, the guideline first-line treatment)<Cite id="bsg-2021-ida" />
- Vitamin C 500 mg daily (optional)
- Check hemoglobin within the first 4 weeks, as UK guidelines advise, and retest ferritin after 6-8 weeks<Cite id="bsg-2021-ida" />

**Timing:** Morning, 2 hours away from calcium supplements and dairy (calcium cuts iron absorption).<Cite id="hallberg-1991-calcium" />

## Who Benefits Most from Lactoferrin

### Pregnant Women

Pregnancy raises the recommended iron intake to 27 mg daily (vs. 18 mg for non-pregnant women aged 19–50).<Cite id="ods-iron" /> In pregnancy trials, lactoferrin matched ferrous sulfate on hemoglobin with fewer GI side effects, though no differences were seen in preterm birth or birthweight.<Cite id="abu-hashim-2017-lactoferrin-pregnancy-meta" /> This is the most evidence-supported use case.

### People with GI Side Effects from Iron

Ferrous sulfate more than doubles the odds of GI side effects such as nausea and constipation compared with placebo.<Cite id="tolkien-2015-ferrous-sulfate-gi" /> In head-to-head trials, lactoferrin caused fewer GI side effects than ferrous sulfate, which may help people who can't tolerate iron.<Cite id="abu-hashim-2017-lactoferrin-pregnancy-meta" /><Cite id="nappi-2009-lactoferrin-pregnancy-rct" />

### Malabsorption Disorders

Conditions like celiac disease (if untreated), Crohn's disease, or post-bariatric surgery reduce iron absorption.<Cite id="bsg-2021-ida" /> Evidence for lactoferrin here is very limited; intravenous iron is the guideline option when absorption is poor.<Cite id="auerbach-2025-iron-deficiency" />

### Vegetarians/Vegans

Iron absorption is about 5–12% from vegetarian diets versus 14–18% from mixed diets.<Cite id="hurrell-egli-2010-bioavailability" /> Whether lactoferrin improves absorption from plant foods in adults hasn't been tested.

### Athletes with Iron Losses

Endurance athletes lose iron through sweat and intestinal blood loss.<Cite id="peeling-2008-athletes" /> In one small trial of 16 female distance runners, adding lactoferrin to iron kept ferritin stable over 8 weeks of summer training while it fell with iron alone — the kind of decline that comes before [hemoglobin drops below normal](/blog/low-ferritin-normal-hemoglobin).<Cite id="koikawa-2008-lactoferrin-runners" />

## Lactoferrin vs. Standard Iron: When to Use What

| Situation | First Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **[Mild deficiency (ferritin 20-40)](/blog/ferritin-30-low-normal)** | Oral iron; lactoferrin if iron isn't tolerated | Guideline first line; lactoferrin is gentler |
| **[Moderate anemia (Hgb 8-10.9)](/blog/hemoglobin-14-normal)** | Ferrous sulfate alone | Guideline first line; strongest evidence |
| **Severe anemia (Hgb &lt;8)** | Ferrous sulfate; consider IV iron | Requires rapid repletion; oral may be too slow |
| **Pregnant woman** | Lactoferrin or iron | Matched iron in small trials; better tolerated |
| **GI intolerance to iron** | Alternate-day iron or lactoferrin | Fewer GI side effects |
| **Malabsorption** | IV iron | Guideline option when absorption is poor; lactoferrin largely untested |

UK and US guidance start with an oral iron salt and move to IV iron when it fails, isn't tolerated, or can't be absorbed; anemia severity follows WHO cut-offs (moderate 8.0–10.9 g/dL, severe below 8.0).<Cite id="bsg-2021-ida" /><Cite id="auerbach-2025-iron-deficiency" /><Cite id="who-2024-hemoglobin" />

**Key insight:** Lactoferrin isn't inherently "better" than ferrous sulfate—they work through different mechanisms and suit different needs. For tolerance, lactoferrin has the edge in trials; for correcting anemia, oral iron salts (or IV iron) remain the guideline standard.<Cite id="abu-hashim-2017-lactoferrin-pregnancy-meta" /><Cite id="bsg-2021-ida" />

## Monitoring: Testing Iron Status

Before and after supplementing, measure:

| Test | Baseline | After 6-8 Weeks | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hemoglobin** | Check | Retest | ≥13.0 g/dL (men); ≥12.0 (women) |
| **Ferritin** | Check | Retest | 30-150 ng/mL (replete) |
| **TIBC, Transferrin saturation** | If deficient | If still low | TIBC back within the lab range; sat ≥20% |

These targets follow the WHO anemia cut-offs, the 30 ng/mL line for low iron stores, and the 20% saturation threshold for iron-restricted red cell production.<Cite id="who-2024-hemoglobin" /><Cite id="bsg-2021-ida" /> If your baseline ferritin is very low — under 15 — read [what a ferritin of 15 means](/blog/ferritin-level-15) before choosing a protocol.

**Interpretation:**
- **Rising hemoglobin and ferritin:** Supplementation is working; continue
- **Stalled hemoglobin despite rising ferritin:** Iron may not be the only cause of the anemia; discuss with doctor
- **No improvement in either:** Possible ongoing blood loss (investigate) or [inadequate absorption](/blog/iron-supplements-not-working)<Cite id="bsg-2021-ida" />

## Safety and Side Effects

Lactoferrin was well tolerated at the 200 mg daily dose used in trials.<Cite id="nappi-2009-lactoferrin-pregnancy-rct" /><Cite id="abu-hashim-2017-lactoferrin-pregnancy-meta" /> Side effects are rare but can include:
- **Mild GI upset** (nausea, bloating) in some people
- **Constipation** (usually from iron component, not lactoferrin)<Cite id="nappi-2009-lactoferrin-pregnancy-rct" />
- **Hypersensitivity** (rare; more likely in people with milk allergies, since it is a cow's milk protein)<Cite id="efsa-2012-bovine-lactoferrin" />

There's no established upper limit of safety for lactoferrin; EFSA judged bovine lactoferrin safe at intakes that, for adults, averaged about 1.4 g a day (3.4 g at the high end), well above supplement doses.<Cite id="efsa-2012-bovine-lactoferrin" />

**Drug interactions:** Not well studied. Lactoferrin is a food-derived milk protein.
