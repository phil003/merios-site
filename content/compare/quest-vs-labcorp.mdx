## Are Quest and Labcorp results comparable?

Mostly, with an important caveat. Both companies are regulated under the same federal framework. Quest states that all its testing locations are certified under CLIA and that its regional and esoteric reference laboratories are [accredited by the College of American Pathologists](https://www.questdiagnostics.com/our-company/about-us/licenses-accreditations). Labcorp publishes [CLIA certificate and CAP participant numbers](https://www.labcorp.com/providers/resources/lab-certifications-accreditations) for its reference and specialty labs. There is no public, peer-reviewed head-to-head showing that one is more accurate than the other across routine tests.

The caveat is that "certified" does not mean "identical." For many tests, the two labs can use different instruments, reagents, calculation methods or units. Each lab then sets or validates its own reference range for its own method. The practical effects:

- **The same blood can give slightly different numbers.** For tightly standardized tests the difference is usually small. For hormones, some vitamins and certain specialty markers, it can be larger.
- **The printed range can move.** A value flagged high at one lab may sit inside the range at the other, or the other way round, because the range itself is different.
- **Calculated values can differ.** Some results, such as estimated LDL or eGFR, depend on which formula the lab uses.

The honest rule: read every result against the reference range printed on that lab's own report, and if you care about trends, use one lab for a given marker. ## Why did my ranges change when I switched labs?

This is one of the most common questions people ask after a move from Quest to Labcorp or back. In most cases nothing went wrong. The new lab is likely using a different method, a different unit, or a range built from a different reference population. Normal day-to-day biological variation, fasting status, time of day, hydration and recent exercise can also shift a value between two draws, whichever lab runs them.

If you switch labs, three habits help:

1. **Write down the lab next to every result.** A jump that coincides with a lab change deserves caution before you read it as a real change.
2. **Compare against each report's own range,** not a range you remember from the previous lab.
3. **Repeat under the same conditions** (same time of day, same fasting status) before drawing conclusions, and discuss any concerning shift with a clinician.

## Prices, self-ordering and the Apple Health option

Both labs let you order many tests yourself without a doctor visit. An independent clinician reviews and authorizes the order behind the scenes, and you go to a patient service center for the draw. If you are an iPhone user who wants a broad baseline, check whether it is live in your state before buying a panel elsewhere.

## Insurance: which one is covered?

There is no general answer, because coverage is set by your plan, not by the lab. Insurers contract with specific laboratories, and a plan may be in network with Quest, with Labcorp, with both, or with a regional or hospital lab instead. Using an out-of-network lab can leave you with a much larger bill for the same test.

Before a doctor-ordered draw, check your insurer's provider directory or call the number on your member card, then ask the office to send the order to that lab. Self-pay orders placed on questhealth.com or Labcorp OnDemand are cash purchases and are usually not submitted to insurance; ask your plan administrator whether HSA or FSA funds can be used.

## Which one should you choose?

- **Your doctor ordered the test and you have insurance:** use whichever lab your plan has in network. This single factor usually outweighs everything else on this page.
- **You are paying yourself for one or two routine tests:** prices are close. Labcorp OnDemand currently lists no separate physician fee on the tests we checked, which makes it slightly cheaper for a single CMP or lipid panel; Quest sometimes discounts individual tests. Check both on the day.
- **You want a broad panel:** compare the marker lists of Quest's profiles and Labcorp's health tests side by side, and look at Quest's Apple Health panel once it is available to you.
- **You already have years of results at one lab:** stay there unless cost or access forces a change. Continuity makes trends easier to read.
- **You care most about convenience:** pick the draw site closest to home or work. Labcorp's draws inside Walgreens stores may help; Quest's MyQuest app links results with Apple Health.
