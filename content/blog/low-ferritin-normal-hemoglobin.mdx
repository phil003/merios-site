You get your blood work back. Hemoglobin? Normal. Red blood cell count? Fine. Your doctor says you're good—but you're still exhausted, your hair is falling out, and climbing stairs feels like climbing a mountain. What's happening?

The answer lies in a single marker that often gets overlooked: **ferritin**.

## Hemoglobin vs. Ferritin: They Tell Different Stories

Your blood test includes hemoglobin and ferritin, but they measure two completely different things:

**Hemoglobin** is the iron-containing protein inside red blood cells that carries oxygen. When hemoglobin is low (below 12 g/dL for women, 13 g/dL for men), you have anemia—not enough oxygen-carrying capacity.<Cite id="who-2024-hemoglobin" />

**Ferritin** is the storage form of iron in your body, primarily in the liver and in the macrophages that recycle old red blood cells.<Cite id="ganz-2013-iron-homeostasis" /> A ferritin test reflects your total iron reserves.<Cite id="who-2020-ferritin" /> When ferritin is low, your body's iron storage is depleted, even if there's still enough iron circulating to maintain normal hemoglobin.

This distinction matters enormously.

## The Iron Depletion Stages

Iron deficiency doesn't happen overnight. It progresses through predictable stages:<Cite id="who-2020-ferritin" />

1. **Iron depletion** — Ferritin drops first (typically below 30 ng/mL), but hemoglobin and all other markers remain normal.<Cite id="bsg-2021-ida" /> This is you: exhausted, but "normal" lab results.

2. **Early iron-deficient erythropoiesis** — Ferritin stays low, hemoglobin is still normal, but other markers (like serum iron or transferrin saturation) begin to shift. Symptoms can build. You can compute yours with the free [transferrin saturation calculator](/tools/transferrin-saturation-calculator).

3. **Iron-deficiency anemia** — Ferritin is low, and now hemoglobin finally drops below normal. Now your doctor will likely act.

**The problem:** Many people go through stage 1 and sometimes stage 2 with no medical acknowledgment, because the focus stays on hemoglobin.<Cite id="al-naseem-2021-idwa" />

## Why Low Ferritin Causes Symptoms Even with Normal Hemoglobin

When your ferritin is low, your iron stores are depleted. Your body is running on fumes. Even though there's still enough circulating iron to make hemoglobin, your cells don't have reserves for:

**Mitochondrial function** — Your mitochondria (the powerhouses of your cells) contain iron-dependent enzymes called cytochromes. These proteins drive energy production.<Cite id="kumar-2022-ida-review" /> Without adequate iron, your cells produce less ATP, the energy currency your body runs on. Result: fatigue even with normal oxygen delivery — in trials, iron reduced fatigue in non-anemic women with low ferritin.<Cite id="vaucher-2012-fatigue-rct" /><Cite id="houston-2018-idna-review" />

**Myoglobin production** — Similar to hemoglobin but in muscle cells, myoglobin is an iron-containing protein that holds oxygen for working muscle.<Cite id="who-2020-ferritin" /> Exercise intolerance is a recognized symptom of iron deficiency, though in trials of non-anemic adults, iron didn't improve objective measures of physical capacity.<Cite id="auerbach-2025-iron-deficiency" /><Cite id="houston-2018-idna-review" />

**Hair growth and skin health** — Hair follicles are metabolically active, and low iron has been linked to shedding and thinning, but studies disagree and there is little evidence that iron helps hair in people without anemia.<Cite id="trost-2006-hair-loss" />

**Immune function** — Immune cells need iron too, and iron-deficiency anemia is linked to impaired immune function.<Cite id="ods-iron" />

**Neurological function** — Iron deficiency impairs brain myelination and the metabolism of neurotransmitters such as dopamine and serotonin, and is associated with poor concentration, irritability, anxiety, and even depression.<Cite id="kim-2014-iron-brain" /><Cite id="auerbach-2025-iron-deficiency" /> Some studies link iron deficiency to ADHD symptoms in children.<Cite id="tseng-2018-adhd" />

**Temperature regulation** — Iron is needed for normal thyroid hormone metabolism. In women with iron-deficiency anemia, body temperature and thyroid hormone levels dropped more in the cold, though women with low iron stores but no anemia responded normally.<Cite id="beard-1990-thermoregulation" />

**Restless leg syndrome** — An often-overlooked symptom. Low ferritin is strongly associated with an irresistible urge to move the legs, especially at night, disrupting sleep.<Cite id="auerbach-2025-iron-deficiency" /><Cite id="aasm-2025-rls" />

## What's Actually "Normal"?

Here's where clinical practice gets messy. Most labs define "normal" ferritin as roughly **13–150 ng/mL for women** and **30–400 ng/mL for men**.<Cite id="medlineplus-ferritin" /> These limits come from reference populations that often included iron-deficient people, not from evidence about what's healthy.<Cite id="truong-2024-ferritin-reference" />

Guidelines and trials point higher than many lab minimums, though none defines an "optimal" level:

- **Women**: in trials, iron eased fatigue in non-anemic women with ferritin *below 50 ng/mL*<Cite id="vaucher-2012-fatigue-rct" /><Cite id="verdon-2003-fatigue-rct" />
- **Men**: the same cut-offs apply — below 30 ng/mL generally means low stores, and the AGA uses 45 ng/mL when anemia is present<Cite id="bsg-2021-ida" /><Cite id="aga-2020-ida" />
- **Athletes**: no sport-specific target is agreed, but iron deficiency is common in athletes, especially women<Cite id="sim-2019-athletes" />

Why the difference? Because ferritin below 30 already signals low stores, and symptoms of low iron stores—fatigue, poor concentration, exercise intolerance—can appear before anemia does.<Cite id="bsg-2021-ida" /><Cite id="auerbach-2025-iron-deficiency" /> By waiting for anemia to develop, you may live with preventable exhaustion for a long time.

## Who's at Risk?

Certain populations are particularly vulnerable to iron depletion:

**Menstruating women** — The biggest risk group. Losing blood monthly means losing iron. Heavy periods (classically defined as 80 mL or more per cycle) accelerate depletion.<Cite id="munro-2023-hmb" /><Cite id="bofill-2020-lng-ius" /> In US national data, about a third of women aged 18–50 had ferritin below 30 ng/mL.<Cite id="tawfik-2024-us-iron-deficiency" />

**Endurance athletes** — Distance runners, cyclists, and triathletes lose iron through sweat, GI bleeding (exercise-induced), and foot-strike hemolysis (red blood cells rupturing from impact).<Cite id="peeling-2008-athletes" /> Female athletes are especially at risk: iron deficiency is found in about 15–35% of female athlete cohorts, versus 5–11% of male ones.<Cite id="sim-2019-athletes" />

**Vegetarians and vegans** — Plant-based iron (non-heme iron) is less bioavailable than heme iron from meat. Absorption is further limited by phytates in grains, calcium, and tannins in tea and coffee.<Cite id="hurrell-egli-2010-bioavailability" /> Vegetarians need roughly 1.8× the iron of meat-eaters.<Cite id="ods-iron" />

**People with GI issues** — Celiac disease, Crohn's disease, and other malabsorption conditions impair iron absorption.<Cite id="bsg-2021-ida" /> Even without obvious GI symptoms, subtle intestinal inflammation can reduce iron uptake.<Cite id="pasricha-2021-iron-deficiency" />

**Frequent blood donors** — Donating blood removes roughly 210–240 mg of iron per donation.<Cite id="mantadakis-2022-blood-donation" /> Regular donors can deplete stores faster than they replenish.<Cite id="ods-iron" />

**People on certain medications** — Some heartburn medications (PPIs) reduce stomach acid and impair iron absorption.<Cite id="lam-2017-ppi" /> NSAIDs can cause chronic GI bleeding.<Cite id="bsg-2021-ida" />

## What Counts as Optimal Ferritin?

If your ferritin is below 30 ng/mL, you should probably investigate why and consider whether you fall into a high-risk group.<Cite id="bsg-2021-ida" /> Symptoms alone warrant testing:<Cite id="auerbach-2025-iron-deficiency" />

- Persistent fatigue despite adequate sleep
- Hair loss or hair that doesn't grow
- Shortness of breath with normal exertion
- Brain fog or poor concentration
- Restless legs at night
- Cold hands and feet
- Frequent infections

## Treating Low Ferritin

**Dietary iron** is the first-line approach:

- **Heme iron sources** (best absorbed): beef, lamb, oysters, clams, liver<Cite id="ods-iron" />
- **Non-heme iron sources**: beans, lentils, dark leafy greens, fortified cereals (absorbed less well, but enhanced by vitamin C; overall, iron absorption is about 5–12% from vegetarian diets versus 14–18% from mixed diets)<Cite id="ods-iron" /><Cite id="hurrell-egli-2010-bioavailability" />
- **Pairing matters**: Eat iron-rich foods with vitamin C (citrus, tomatoes, peppers) to boost absorption. Avoid calcium, coffee, tea, and whole grains with iron-rich meals, as they inhibit absorption.<Cite id="hurrell-egli-2010-bioavailability" />

**Supplemental iron** is often necessary:

- Ferrous sulfate is cheapest but causes GI upset in many people.<Cite id="tolkien-2015-ferrous-sulfate-gi" />
- Ferrous bisglycinate (chelated form) is gentler on the stomach.<Cite id="fischer-2023-bisglycinate" />
- Standard dosing: 50–100 mg elemental iron (one standard tablet) daily or every other day, taken on an empty stomach; adding orange juice or vitamin C hasn't been shown to improve results.<Cite id="bsg-2021-ida" /><Cite id="auerbach-2025-iron-deficiency" /><Cite id="li-2020-vitamin-c" />
- Common side effects: constipation, nausea, dark stools (expected).<Cite id="ods-iron" /><Cite id="bsg-2021-ida" />
- Retest ferritin after 8–12 weeks of supplementation. Goal: at least back above 30 ng/mL, the usual line for low stores, depending on your situation.<Cite id="bsg-2021-ida" />

**Important caveat**: Before supplementing iron, confirm that low ferritin is actually the problem (not another cause of fatigue) and that you don't have hemochromatosis (genetic iron overload), which runs in families and would be worsened by supplementation.<Cite id="easl-2022-haemochromatosis" /><Cite id="trost-2006-hair-loss" />

## The Bottom Line

If you're exhausted and your doctor says your blood work is "normal," ask them to check your ferritin specifically and discuss the functional reference ranges, not just the lab-wide normal range. Normal hemoglobin does not rule out iron deficiency.<Cite id="al-naseem-2021-idwa" /> Iron depletion happens in stages, and stage 1—where ferritin is low but hemoglobin is still normal—is where most preventable exhaustion happens.

And if your ferritin comes back inside the range while you still feel depleted, the next number to ask for is transferrin saturation: [iron saturation and ferritin measure different things](/blog/ferritin-vs-iron-saturation), and a normal ferritin paired with a low saturation is a recognised pattern rather than a contradiction.<Cite id="tawfik-2024-us-iron-deficiency" />

Don't accept fatigue as normal. Iron is too important to your mitochondria, your immune system, and your brain.
