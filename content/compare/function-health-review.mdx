**Function Health is a solid buy for a healthy adult who wants about 160 lab tests a year, drawn at Quest, explained in plain language, and who is happy to pay $365 out of pocket for it.** It is a weaker buy if your doctor already orders a yearly panel through insurance, if you expect a physician to walk you through every result, or if you live in Rhode Island, Hawaii or Puerto Rico, where it is not offered.

This is not a first-person test. We did not buy a membership to write this page. Instead, it lays out what you get, what it costs, what it does well, where it falls short and who it fits, based on Function Health's own pages, every one of them **checked November 25, 2026** and linked inline. Prices change often, and Function was running a Black Friday promotion on the day we checked, so confirm on the site before you pay.

## What you get for $365

The core of the membership is lab testing, done twice a year. The first visit runs 100+ tests and the second, 3 to 6 months later, runs 60+, for 160+ in total, according to the [Function FAQ](https://www.functionhealth.com/faq). Function groups them by area, such as heart and metabolic health, hormones and thyroid, aging and silent risks, on its [home page](https://www.functionhealth.com/). You fast for 8 hours before the draw.

Results land in your Function account. Each one comes with a clinician-reviewed explanation, plus a summary and a personalized action plan. You can download the results as a PDF to share with your own doctor ([Function FAQ](https://www.functionhealth.com/faq)). The membership also bundles member pricing on MRI and CT scans through Ezra and a supplement buying club ([pricing](https://www.functionhealth.com/pricing)).

What you do not get is insurance billing. Function says it is 100% insurance-free. The membership is listed as HSA/FSA eligible, and members receive an itemized receipt to submit for reimbursement.

## Does a doctor actually follow up on your results?

This is one of the most common questions buyers ask, and the answer is narrower than the marketing suggests. Function says a healthcare provider will call you right away if any result is critical. Beyond that, the [FAQ](https://www.functionhealth.com/faq) describes clinician-reviewed notes, not routine calls or visits. Function also describes itself as a healthcare technology company, not a laboratory or medical provider.

In practice, that means Function is a testing and interpretation service. If a result is borderline, or if you want to start a medication, the next step is still your own clinician. Bring the PDF. If you do not have a regular doctor, factor that gap in before you buy.

## How fast are results, and is the second draw worth it?

Function's FAQ says first results typically come within 48 hours of the draw. Function describes that as the first results, so expect the rest of the panel to fill in afterward rather than all at once.

The mid-year retest is where opinions differ most. It is a smaller panel (60+ tests, versus 100+ at the start), and for someone whose first results were all in range, a second look a few months later may add little. It earns its keep when the first draw flagged something you are working on, such as LDL cholesterol, ApoB, A1c, ferritin or vitamin D, because a repeat test shows whether the change you made is moving the number. A single value is a snapshot; two or three over time are what make a trend readable. Our guide on [how to read blood test results](/blog/how-to-read-blood-test-results) explains how to judge a change between two draws.

## What happens after the first year?

The membership renews every year. If you joined on a promotional first-year price, renewal is at the standard rate, which was $365 on November 25, 2026 ([pricing](https://www.functionhealth.com/pricing)). The [FAQ](https://www.functionhealth.com/faq) says you can cancel and rejoin at any time, but you cannot pause, and Function does not refund prior payments. If you only want one year, set a reminder a few weeks before your renewal date.

Your results stay downloadable as PDFs, so you can keep your own record whether or not you renew. That matters more than it sounds: the long-term value of bloodwork comes from comparing this year with next year, not from one big panel.

## Function Health vs asking your own doctor

Many people weighing Function already see a primary care doctor once a year. A standard physical often includes a basic metabolic or comprehensive metabolic panel, a lipid panel and a CBC, and insurance usually covers them when your doctor orders them. Function's pitch is breadth: tests a routine physical may not include, such as ApoB, Lp(a), a fuller thyroid panel, hormones, inflammation markers and nutrients, without having to argue for each one.

Two honest points cut the other way. First, more tests mean more chances of a result slightly outside the range, which can lead to worry or extra follow-up testing that may not change anything. Second, your doctor knows your history and can order targeted tests that matter for you, and act on them. If you mainly want two or three specific markers, ordering them yourself from Quest or Labcorp may cost far less than a membership; our [Quest vs Labcorp guide](/compare/quest-vs-labcorp) lists self-pay prices, and [getting a blood test without a doctor](/blog/blood-test-without-doctor) explains how it works.

## Function Health pros and cons

**Pros**

- **Breadth for the price.** 160+ tests a year for $365 is a wide panel compared with ordering the same markers one by one.
- **Quest draws.** 2,000+ Quest locations make a draw easy to find in most of the US, with an at-home option.
- **Readable results.** Clinician-reviewed notes and an action plan turn a long lab report into something a non-specialist can follow.
- **Fast first results.** Typically within 48 hours, per Function.
- **HSA/FSA eligible.** You can pay with pre-tax money, subject to your plan's rules.

**Cons**

- **No insurance billing.** If your plan would cover a yearly panel, you may be paying for tests you could get covered.
- **Limited clinician contact.** A call is promised for critical results; routine questions go to your own doctor.
- **Add-on prices are not public.** Extra panels cost a member-only price that is not listed on the pages we checked.
- **Not everywhere.** Unavailable in Rhode Island, Hawaii and Puerto Rico, and more expensive in New York and New Jersey.
- **Renewal terms.** Annual auto-renewal at full price, no pauses and no refunds for prior payments.
- **Risk of over-testing.** A very wide panel may surface borderline results that are associated with worry more than with a real problem.

## Who Function Health is for, and not for

**A good fit if you:**

- Are a generally healthy adult who wants a broad baseline and a repeat check a few months later.
- Want markers your routine physical does not include, and are willing to pay cash for them.
- Will actually change something, such as diet, training, sleep or a talk with your doctor, based on the results.

**Probably not for you if you:**

- Already get a yearly panel through insurance and mainly want a few extra tests (order those directly).
- Need ongoing care for a diagnosed condition, are pregnant, or are under 18.
- Live in Rhode Island, Hawaii or Puerto Rico.
- Find long lists of results stressful rather than useful.
