## Is HbA1c 6.0% High?

**Yes — 6.0% is solidly prediabetic and sits closer to diabetes than to normal.** The American Diabetes Association draws the lines at: normal below 5.7%, prediabetes 5.7–6.4%, and diabetes at 6.5% and above.<Cite id="ada-2025-diagnosis" /> 6.0% also counts as high risk under the narrower 6.0–6.4% range used by the UK's NICE.<Cite id="huang-2016-prediabetes-cvd" /> At 6.0% you are in the upper half of the prediabetic band, which means your glucose control has already drifted meaningfully — but you are still well inside the range that lifestyle change can reverse.

## Where Does 6.0% Fall?

| Category | HbA1c (%) |
|----------|-----------|
| Optimal | &lt;5.5 |
| Normal | 5.5–5.6 |
| Prediabetes (lower) | 5.7–5.9 |
| **Your value (6.0%)** | **Prediabetes (upper)** |
| Prediabetes (upper) | 6.0–6.4 |
| Diabetes | &gt;=6.5 |

The "optimal" band reflects cohort data in which adults below 5.5% had the lowest risk of developing diabetes and heart disease.<Cite id="selvin-2010-a1c-aric" />

## What 6.0% Means for Your Average Blood Sugar

HbA1c measures the percentage of your hemoglobin that has glucose attached, averaged over roughly the past 2–3 months (red blood cells live about 120 days).<Cite id="ada-2025-diagnosis" /> Convert it to an estimated average glucose (eAG) with the standard formula: eAG = 28.7 × A1C − 46.7.<Cite id="nathan-2008-adag" /> Run your own number with the free [A1C to blood sugar calculator](/tools/a1c-calculator).

At 6.0%, your eAG is about **126 mg/dL**. Because that is a 24-hour average, your after-meal readings are almost certainly higher, especially on refined-carb meals. Those spikes, not just the average, matter: in pooled European cohorts, glucose two hours after a glucose drink predicted death better than fasting glucose did.<Cite id="decode-1999-glucose-mortality" />

## Why It Matters Before Diabetes

Prediabetes is not a benign waiting room. Even at 6.0%, the elevated glucose is already associated with rising cardiovascular risk,<Cite id="huang-2016-prediabetes-cvd" /> and roughly 5–10% of people with prediabetes progress to type 2 diabetes each year if nothing changes<Cite id="tabak-2012-prediabetes" /> — for an A1C of 6.0–6.5%, a systematic review put the 5-year risk at 25–50%.<Cite id="zhang-2010-a1c-diabetes-risk" /> The upside: caught at 6.0%, it is one of the most reversible findings on a standard panel. Not every adult-onset diabetes is type 2 — [how LADA is distinguished on blood work](/blog/type-2-diabetes-vs-lada-blood-test) covers the antibody testing that separates them.

## How to Bring 6.0% Down

- **Lose 5–7% of body weight if you carry excess fat.** This is the single biggest lever — weight loss was the main driver of the Diabetes Prevention Program's 58% cut in progression, achieved by a lifestyle program that also included 150 minutes a week of activity.<Cite id="knowler-2002-dpp" /><Cite id="hamman-2006-dpp-weight-loss" />
- **Walk for 10–15 minutes after meals.** Post-meal movement blunts the glucose spike that A1C is averaging.<Cite id="reynolds-2016-post-meal-walking" />
- **Cut refined carbs and sugar-sweetened drinks first.** Swap toward protein, fiber, and whole foods; you do not need zero carbs, just fewer fast ones.<Cite id="evert-2019-nutrition-consensus" />
- **Protect your sleep.** A single short night raises next-day insulin resistance;<Cite id="donga-2010-sleep-insulin" /> chronic short sleep is linked to higher A1C in people with type 2 diabetes.<Cite id="lee-2017-sleep-glycemic-control" />
- **Pair A1C with fasting insulin.** A1C tells you the result; [fasting insulin](/blog/fasting-insulin-levels-chart) and [HOMA-IR](/blog/homa-ir-insulin-resistance) tell you how hard your body is working to get there — often the earliest warning.<Cite id="dankner-2009-hyperinsulinemia" />

## When to Retest

Because A1C is a rolling 3-month average, retest after about **12 weeks** of consistent change — testing sooner will only partly reflect your effort.<Cite id="tahara-1995-hba1c-kinetics" /> A drop of a few tenths of a point is a realistic goal over the next several months: DPP-style lifestyle programs lowered A1C by about 0.2 points on average.<Cite id="mudaliar-2016-dpp-programs" />
