## Is ALT 40 U/L Normal or High?

**Technically normal, but above optimal.** The traditional lab range for ALT (alanine aminotransferase) is 7–56 U/L, which puts 40 squarely in the "normal" box. However, newer research — now reflected in the American College of Gastroenterology's 2017 guideline — puts the truly healthy upper limit at about **&lt;25 U/L for women and &lt;33 U/L for men**, and says levels above this should be assessed.<Cite id="kwo-2017-acg-liver-chemistries" /> At 40, your liver is functioning, but the level sits above that healthy range, signaling potential metabolic stress.

## Where Does 40 U/L Fall?

| Category | ALT Range (U/L) |
|----------|-----------------|
| Optimal (modern standards) | &lt;25 (women), &lt;33 (men) |
| **Your value (40)** | **Above optimal** |
| Normal (old lab range) | 7–56 |
| Mildly elevated | 56–100 |
| Moderately elevated | 100–300 |
| Severely elevated | &gt;300 |

These bands are a rough guide. The American College of Gastroenterology grades elevations by multiples of the upper limit of normal instead: borderline (under 2x), mild (2–5x), moderate (5–15x) and severe (above 15x).<Cite id="kwo-2017-acg-liver-chemistries" />

### What ALT Measures

ALT is an enzyme found primarily in liver cells. When liver cells are damaged or inflamed, they release ALT into the bloodstream.<Cite id="kwo-2017-acg-liver-chemistries" /> A slightly elevated ALT signals that your liver is under metabolic stress — often from fatty liver, inflammation, or metabolic dysfunction — even if you don't have hepatitis or cirrhosis.

## Why "Normal" Range Is Outdated

The traditional upper limit of normal (56 U/L) was set decades ago and wasn't specifically chosen for optimal health: lab reference populations included people with undiagnosed fatty liver and other silent liver disease.<Cite id="prati-2002-alt-healthy-ranges" /><Cite id="rinella-2023-aasld-nafld-guidance" /> Modern data shows:

- **Fatty liver disease (MASLD, formerly NAFLD):** Often presents with normal or mildly elevated ALT (30–60). Many people with fatty liver think their liver is fine because their ALT is "normal."<Cite id="rinella-2023-aasld-nafld-guidance" />
- **Metabolic syndrome:** Elevated ALT correlates with insulin resistance, even before frank liver disease.<Cite id="vozarova-2002-alt-insulin-resistance" />
- **Cardiovascular risk:** Unlike GGT, ALT on its own has not been consistently linked to heart disease or stroke in pooled data from 29 cohort studies — treat it mainly as a liver and metabolic signal.<Cite id="kunutsor-2014-liver-enzymes-cvd" />
- **Optimal health:** Most healthy, lean people have ALT &lt;25 (women) or &lt;33 (men).<Cite id="prati-2002-alt-healthy-ranges" />

At 40, you may be in the early stages of metabolic or liver stress that's not yet "diseased" but is visible at the cellular level.

## Common Causes of ALT 40

**Fatty liver disease (MASLD, formerly called nonalcoholic fatty liver disease or NAFLD):** The #1 cause in developed countries.<Cite id="clark-2003-elevated-aminotransferases" /><Cite id="rinella-2023-masld-nomenclature" /> Fat accumulates in liver cells without cirrhosis, causing mild inflammation and elevated ALT. Risk factors: obesity, insulin resistance, metabolic syndrome, [type 2 diabetes](/blog/hba1c-5-7-pre-diabetic).<Cite id="rinella-2023-aasld-nafld-guidance" />

**Excess alcohol:** Even 2–3 drinks daily — at or above what liver guidelines define as significant drinking — can cause fatty liver and elevated ALT.<Cite id="alatalo-2008-alcohol-bmi-liver-enzymes" /><Cite id="kwo-2017-acg-liver-chemistries" />

**Medications:** Acetaminophen (especially if chronic use or in combination with alcohol), NSAIDs, statins, antibiotics, and others can elevate ALT.<Cite id="kwo-2017-acg-liver-chemistries" />

**Viral hepatitis:** Less common but possible if exposed. Chronic hepatitis C in particular usually causes no symptoms for years, so a normal-feeling body doesn't rule it out.<Cite id="schillie-2020-cdc-hcv-screening" />

**Metabolic syndrome:** High blood sugar, [high triglycerides](/blog/how-to-lower-triglycerides), [insulin resistance](/blog/homa-ir-insulin-resistance), and obesity all promote liver inflammation.

**Autoimmune hepatitis:** Rare; diagnosed with antibody tests (such as ANA and anti-smooth muscle antibodies), immunoglobulin levels and usually a liver biopsy.<Cite id="kwo-2017-acg-liver-chemistries" />

**Hemochromatosis or other metabolic disorders:** Iron or copper overload can damage the liver.<Cite id="kwo-2017-acg-liver-chemistries" />

## Do You Have Symptoms?

ALT 40 usually causes no symptoms. Your liver is quiet — damage happens silently until it's advanced. However, watch for:

- **Fatigue or brain fog:** Common with metabolic liver disease.<Cite id="newton-2008-nafld-fatigue" />
- **Abdominal bloating or right-sided discomfort:** May indicate liver inflammation.
- **Jaundice (yellowing of skin/eyes):** Sign of advanced liver dysfunction.
- **Easy bruising or bleeding:** Liver dysfunction impairs clotting.

If you have any of these with ALT 40, notify your doctor immediately.

## What to Do Next

**1. Get liver context:**
Ask your doctor to check:
- **AST (aspartate aminotransferase):** Another liver enzyme. The AST/ALT ratio indicates type of damage.
- **Bilirubin:** Elevated (conjugated) bilirubin suggests more significant liver disease or a bile-flow problem.
- **ALP (alkaline phosphatase):** Elevated ALP alongside ALT suggests a cholestatic (bile-flow) or mixed pattern, such as bile duct obstruction.
- **GGT (gamma-glutamyl transferase):** a marker that rises with fatty liver and alcohol, and confirms whether a high ALP comes from the liver — see [what high GGT means](/blog/ggt-levels-high-meaning).
- **Albumin and total protein:** Assess liver synthetic function.<Cite id="kwo-2017-acg-liver-chemistries" />
- **Ultrasound or FibroScan:** If ALT is persistently elevated, imaging can detect fatty liver or fibrosis.<Cite id="rinella-2023-aasld-nafld-guidance" />

**2. Check for viral hepatitis:**
The CDC now recommends hepatitis B and C screening at least once for every adult, with additional testing if you have risk factors (IV drug use, multiple sex partners, tattoos, healthcare needle exposure). Ask for hepatitis B and C testing if you haven't had it.<Cite id="schillie-2020-cdc-hcv-screening" /><Cite id="conners-2023-cdc-hbv-screening" />

**3. Assess alcohol consumption:**
- If you drink more than 1–2 drinks daily, this is likely contributing. Cut back to &lt;1 drink daily or abstain for 8–12 weeks, then retest.<Cite id="alatalo-2008-alcohol-bmi-liver-enzymes" />

**4. Optimize diet (highest impact):**
- **Cut refined carbs and sugar:** These drive fatty liver. Remove soda, juice, pastries, white bread, and candy.<Cite id="rinella-2023-aasld-nafld-guidance" />
- **Eat whole foods:** Vegetables, lean proteins, whole grains, healthy fats.
- **Add coffee:** 3–4 cups daily of coffee correlates with lower ALT and a lower risk of fibrosis and cirrhosis.<Cite id="xiao-2014-coffee-liver-enzymes" /><Cite id="rinella-2023-aasld-nafld-guidance" />
- **Increase fiber:** Soluble fiber (oats, beans, vegetables) supports liver health.<Cite id="rinella-2023-aasld-nafld-guidance" />
- **Limit fructose:** High-fructose corn syrup and excess fruit juice drive fatty liver.<Cite id="softic-2016-fructose-lipogenesis" />

**5. Exercise (improves insulin sensitivity):**
- 150 minutes weekly of moderate cardio improves fatty liver, and aerobic exercise lowers ALT even without weight loss.<Cite id="rinella-2023-aasld-nafld-guidance" /><Cite id="babu-2021-exercise-nafld" />
- Aim for 7,000–10,000 steps daily.

**6. Lose weight if overweight:**
- Even a 5–10% weight loss significantly lowers ALT and improves liver fat.<Cite id="koutoukidis-2019-weight-loss-nafld" />

**7. Manage medications:**
- Review all over-the-counter and prescription medications with your doctor. Acetaminophen, NSAIDs, and many others can elevate ALT. Ask if alternatives exist.
- Avoid herbal supplements like kava, comfrey, and excessive vitamin A.<Cite id="livertox-kava" /><Cite id="stickel-2000-comfrey" /><Cite id="livertox-vitamin-a" />

**8. Retest ALT in 8–12 weeks:**
- After lifestyle changes, retest ALT.
- Goal: Bring it below 33 (men) or 25 (women).
- If ALT is rising or not improving, discuss further workup (imaging, specialist referral).

## Red Flags: When to Escalate

Contact your doctor immediately if:
- ALT jumps above 200 U/L (suggests acute damage).<Cite id="kwo-2017-acg-liver-chemistries" />
- You develop jaundice, dark urine, or pale stools (liver dysfunction signs).
- You have ALT &gt;40 + elevated AST (AST &gt; ALT suggests cirrhosis risk).<Cite id="kwo-2017-acg-liver-chemistries" />
- You have ascites (abdominal swelling) or edema (leg swelling).
