**Superpower is a good value for a healthy adult who wants about 100 blood markers once a year, drawn at Quest, explained in an app, for $199 (or $179 on the Black Friday offer we saw), and who does not expect a doctor to own the follow-up.** It is a weaker buy if insurance already covers a yearly panel for you, if you want a clinician to interpret each result, or if you live in one of the states Superpower does not serve.

This is not a first-person test. We did not buy a membership to write this page. It sets out what you get, what it costs, how the process works, what it does well and where people complain, based on Superpower's own pages, each one **checked November 27, 2026** and linked inline, plus a fair summary of public reviews. Superpower changes prices often and was running a Black Friday sale on the day we checked, so confirm before you pay.

## What you get for $199

The membership is built around one comprehensive blood draw a year: 100+ biomarkers covering heart and metabolic health, hormones and thyroid, iron and energy, nutrients and inflammation, according to the [pricing page](https://superpower.com/pricing). Around that draw, the same page lists a biological age and 17 health scores, a personalized plan covering diet, lifestyle and supplements, an AI chat with context on your data, wearable sync with Apple Health, Whoop and Oura, and the option to upload past labs.

New members also get a complimentary follow-up test, which Superpower values at $99, auto-added at checkout and meant to be taken 3 to 6 months after the first draw ([FAQ](https://superpower.com/faqs)). It is a new-member benefit, not a standing second draw every year.

Everything else costs extra. Superpower lists add-on tests such as gut microbiome, environmental toxins and heavy metals, and the Galleri cancer screen, plus peptides, supplements and prescriptions through its marketplace ([pricing](https://superpower.com/pricing), [FAQ](https://superpower.com/faqs)). The pages we checked did not show prices for most of these, so budget for them separately if you expect to use them.

## Price on November 27, 2026, and what happens at renewal

The standard rate was **$199 a year**. The Black Friday banner showed **$179**, and the Blood + Genome bundle was **$299** instead of $498. None of the pages we opened gave an end date for the sale. Superpower says pricing may vary in New York and New Jersey without listing the figure; Athletech's review, published November 23, 2026, reported [$399 for New York and New Jersey, or $379 on its Black Friday offer](https://athletechnews.com/superpower-blood-test-review/), which we could not confirm on superpower.com.

The renewal term matters more than the sale. Superpower's [Membership Agreement](https://superpower.com/legal/membership), last updated November 9, 2026, says the membership renews automatically at the end of each term at the **then-current, non-promotional rate**, and that you must cancel before your renewal date to avoid the charge. So a $179 first year renews at whatever the list price is a year from now, $199 today. After you cancel, you keep read-only access to your data (Membership Agreement, section 14(c)).

## Can you get a refund?

This is the question the top reviews skip, and Superpower's own pages disagree. The [FAQ](https://superpower.com/faqs) said: Superpower does not offer refunds at this time. The [Membership Agreement](https://superpower.com/legal/membership), which governs your membership, describes a **Satisfaction Refund**:

- 100% of the base membership fee for your first year.
- You must ask by the earlier of 7 days after your first results appear in your account, or 45 days after your start date.
- Once per member, first term only, and not for memberships obtained through an employer, a gift or another third party.
- Add-on panels, marketplace purchases and at-home draw fees are not covered.

There is one more refund case in the FAQ: if your HSA or FSA plan declines reimbursement, Superpower says it will refund you before your first lab test. If a refund matters to you, read the agreement in full on the day you join, and take a screenshot.

## How it works: booking, draw, lab and results

You book through the app. The [how-it-works page](https://superpower.com/how-it-works) says the draw can happen at home or at one of about 2,000 Quest Diagnostics locations, and the pricing page describes a 10-minute lab visit.

Three details from the [Membership Agreement](https://superpower.com/legal/membership) are worth knowing before you book:

- **Superpower does not run a lab.** Testing is performed by Labcorp, Quest Diagnostics or another independent laboratory.
- **Superpower does not draw blood itself.** At-home draws are done by independent third-party phlebotomy services.
- **At-home draws carry a fee** disclosed at booking. You can reschedule an at-home appointment free of charge up to 72 hours before; inside that window, a missed or cancelled appointment is non-refundable and the fee is charged. Lab appointments can be rescheduled up to the appointment time at no charge.

Results land in the app. The [FAQ](https://superpower.com/faqs) says the annual panel takes about 1 week, the [pricing page](https://superpower.com/pricing) says 10 days or less, and the follow-up test is listed at 5 to 10 days. BGR's [May 2026 review](https://bgr.com/2184153/superpower-health-review) reported about one to two weeks in practice.

## Are the 100+ biomarkers really 100?

Superpower's count includes more than direct lab measurements. The "What we test" list on its [pricing page](https://superpower.com/pricing) counts calculated items such as the ferritin-to-albumin ratio and calculated LDL cholesterol, and lists its own Health Score, Pace of Aging and BioAge, as well as BMI, as biomarkers. Calculated ratios can be useful, but they are arithmetic on values already measured, not extra information from your blood.

That point was at the center of a false-advertising lawsuit Function Health filed against Superpower in January 2026, settled in June 2026 without any ruling on the merits. We cover it in detail in [Function Health vs Superpower](/compare/function-health-vs-superpower). BGR's reviewer described one practical effect: a ratio flagged as high while both underlying values were in range. The takeaway is simple: judge the panel by the markers you care about, such as ApoB, Lp(a), A1c, thyroid or ferritin, not by the headline number.

## Does a doctor actually look at your results?

Superpower's [FAQ](https://superpower.com/faqs) says its providers include licensed physicians, nurse practitioners and pharmacists, and the pricing page advertises 24/7 access to the care team. The legal pages are narrower. The [Membership Agreement](https://superpower.com/legal/membership) says the concierge service is for operational support and general educational guidance and does not provide medical advice; that you are responsible for checking your results and following up with your primary care provider; and that providers are not notified when you upload your own data, so you should not assume uploads are reviewed. The [Terms](https://superpower.com/legal/terms) describe Superpower as a technology company, not a healthcare provider.

In practice, Superpower is a testing and interpretation app with optional clinical services, not a primary care replacement. If a result looks off, bring it to your own clinician; our guide on [how to read blood test results](/blog/how-to-read-blood-test-results) explains what to look at first.

## Is Superpower available in your state?

Check before you pay, because Superpower's own lists did not match on November 27, 2026. The [FAQ](https://superpower.com/faqs) said it was not yet available in Alaska, Arkansas, Hawaii, Louisiana, Mississippi, North Dakota, Rhode Island, South Dakota and Wyoming. The "States we are live in" list on the [pricing page](https://superpower.com/pricing) included Alaska, Arkansas, Louisiana, Mississippi and Rhode Island, but left out Maine, Montana, New Hampshire, Vermont and West Virginia. Hawaii, North Dakota, South Dakota and Wyoming were missing from both. The FAQ also says Superpower can prescribe in every state where it is live except Alabama and West Virginia.

## What Superpower does well

- **Low entry price.** $199 a year, $179 on the sale we saw, is among the cheapest ways to get a 100-marker panel with an app on top.
- **Easy logistics.** Quest locations across most of the country, with an at-home option.
- **A readable app.** Each marker comes with an explanation and ranges; Athletech's reviewer noted that the downloadable PDF helps when sharing results with a doctor, and called Superpower a complement to a primary care physician, not a replacement.
- **Wearable and past-lab context.** Apple Health, Whoop and Oura sync, plus uploads of older labs, so results are not seen in isolation.
- **A real, if narrow, refund window** in year one, per the Membership Agreement.

## Limits and complaints, summarized fairly

On [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/superpower.com), Superpower showed a TrustScore of 4.4 from about 340 reviews when we checked on November 27, 2026, with most reviews at 5 stars and about 10% at 1 star. Superpower's own pages displayed 4.5 and 4.6. Superpower replies to a share of negative reviews. Among the 1 to 3 star reviews visible when we checked, themes included:

- **Support.** Hard to reach a person; replies by text, slower than expected.
- **Draw problems.** Limited appointment availability, a sample that failed processing and needed a redraw, the wrong sample type collected, and an at-home draw fee one reviewer did not expect.
- **Billing.** One reviewer reported a charge after cancelling and a cumbersome cancellation. Given auto-renewal, set a reminder.
- **Feature changes.** Some reviewers complained about limits added to the AI chat during a membership.
- **Recommendations.** One reviewer said the suggested retest panels did not cover the markers they cared about. BGR also found the sign-up flow pushed upgrades.

Press reviews were mixed in a similar way. [BGR](https://bgr.com/2184153/superpower-health-review) praised the breadth and price but found the onboarding pushy, the protocol closely tied to buying products, and scores like biological age a possible source of anxiety. [Athletech](https://athletechnews.com/superpower-blood-test-review/) was more positive and noted its review contains affiliate links. None of this is proof of a systemic problem; it reflects individual reviews we read.

## Who Superpower fits, and who it does not

**A good fit if you:**

- Are a generally healthy adult who wants a broad yearly baseline at a low cash price.
- Like an app-first experience with AI explanations and wearable data in one place.
- Will act on results, ideally with your own doctor.

**Probably not for you if you:**

- Already get a yearly panel through insurance and want only a few extra markers; ordering them directly may cost less, as our [Quest vs Labcorp guide](/compare/quest-vs-labcorp) shows.
- Want a clinician to own interpretation and follow-up, or need care for a diagnosed condition.
- Live in Hawaii, North Dakota, South Dakota or Wyoming, or another state on the FAQ's not-yet list.
- Find long lists of flagged results stressful.

Superpower sells you a draw. Many people already have results, from Superpower, Function, a Quest or Labcorp self-order, or their doctor. What helps next is seeing how those numbers move between draws, and how they line up with sleep, resting heart rate, HRV and activity.

Merios is for people who already have blood test results and want to understand and track them over time next to their Apple Watch and Apple Health data. It is a preventive health platform for iPhone that does not sell lab tests: Merios Plus scans a lab report you already have, from Superpower or any other lab, and tracks 160+ biomarkers with trends, history and PhenoAge biological age. The free Daily tier covers the Merios Score, activity, recovery and zen, and manual marker entry; Plus costs $44 per year with a 3-day free trial on the annual plan, or $12.99 per month. It runs on iOS 15.1 or later and is available on the [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/merios/id6760352598?pt=128637372&ct=web-superpower-review&mt=8) in 174 countries and regions.
