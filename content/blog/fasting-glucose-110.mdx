## Is Fasting Glucose 110 mg/dL High? Run your own number with the free [A1C to blood sugar calculator](/tools/a1c-calculator).

**Yes — 110 mg/dL is mid-range prediabetes.** A fasting glucose (measured after 8+ hours without food) is read as: normal below 100, prediabetes 100–125, and diabetes at 126 or above. It is also exactly where the WHO's narrower impaired fasting glucose range (110–125 mg/dL) begins.<Cite id="ada-2025-diagnosis" /> At 110 you're halfway through the prediabetic band — past the early-warning edge of 100, but with real room to reverse before it climbs toward the diabetes line.<Cite id="tabak-2012-prediabetes" /> Not every adult-onset diabetes is type 2 — [how LADA is distinguished on blood work](/blog/type-2-diabetes-vs-lada-blood-test) covers the antibody testing that separates them.

## What a High Fasting Number Is Telling You

Fasting glucose is your body's baseline after an overnight fast — it should be low, because you haven't eaten. When it sits at 110, it usually means your liver is releasing glucose overnight faster than your insulin can clear it: early insulin resistance. That's different from a post-meal spike, which is driven more by insulin resistance in muscle.<Cite id="abdul-ghani-2006-ifg-igt" />

## How 110 Relates to A1C

Fasting glucose is one moment; [A1C](/tools/a1c-calculator) is a 2–3 month average.<Cite id="ada-2025-diagnosis" /> They often disagree: in US national survey data, only about a quarter of adults with impaired fasting glucose also had a prediabetic A1C (5.7–6.4%).<Cite id="mann-2010-a1c-ifg" /> If your fasting glucose is 110 but your A1C is normal, your overnight number may be the early signal; if A1C is higher, daytime spikes are adding up. Checking both gives the fuller picture.

## How to Bring 110 Back Under 100

- **Close your eating window earlier.** In a small trial in men with prediabetes, eating within an early 6-hour window (dinner before 3 p.m.) improved insulin sensitivity, even without weight loss.<Cite id="sutton-2018-etrf" />
- **Walk after your evening meal.** Ten minutes meaningfully blunts the post-dinner glucose rise — by 22% in one trial in type 2 diabetes.<Cite id="reynolds-2016-post-meal-walking" />
- **Trim late-night refined carbs.** In a crossover trial, the same dinner eaten at 10 p.m. instead of 6 p.m. kept glucose higher overnight.<Cite id="gu-2020-late-dinner" />
- **Lose 5–7% of body weight if you carry excess.** The most reliable lever for impaired fasting glucose.<Cite id="hamman-2006-dpp-weight-loss" />
- **Check fasting insulin alongside it.** A fasting glucose of 110 with high [fasting insulin](/blog/fasting-insulin-levels-chart) points to insulin resistance; run the [HOMA-IR calculator](/tools/homa-ir-calculator) to quantify it.<Cite id="matthews-1985-homa" />

## When to Retest

Recheck fasting glucose after **4–8 weeks** of consistent change, and pair it with an A1C at the 3-month mark for the rolling average. A single elevated reading can also reflect stress or illness — so one 110 is a flag, not a verdict.<Cite id="rizza-1982-cortisol" />
