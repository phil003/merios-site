High hematocrit means red blood cells take up more of your blood than your lab's reference range, which for adults usually means a result above about 48% to 54% in men or above about 43% to 48% in women. MedlinePlus lists 37% to 48% for men and 34% to 43% for women, while a StatPearls review gives 40% to 54% and 36% to 48%, so the exact cutoff depends on the lab that printed your report.<Cite id="medlineplus-hematocrit" /><Cite id="statpearls-2024-cbc" />

A single high reading is often associated with dehydration, smoking, altitude, sleep apnea or testosterone therapy.<Cite id="medlineplus-hematocrit" /><Cite id="mithoowani-2020-erythrocytosis" /><Cite id="calof-2005-testosterone-adverse-events" /> A result that stays above 49% in men or 48% in women is one of the criteria clinicians consider for a bone marrow condition called polycythemia vera, which is why it is worth rechecking and discussing with your clinician rather than ignoring.<Cite id="icc-2022-myeloid" />

## What Is Hematocrit?

Hematocrit measures the percentage of your blood composed of red blood cells (RBCs). When you get a [complete blood count (CBC)](/blog/cbc-blood-test-results-explained), hematocrit is reported as a percentage—for example, 45% hematocrit means red blood cells occupy 45% of your total blood volume, with the remaining 55% being plasma and other components.<Cite id="billett-1990-hemoglobin-hematocrit" />

This simple measure tells you a lot. It reflects your body's oxygen-carrying capacity, your bone marrow's ability to produce RBCs, and your overall blood health. Both unusually high and unusually low hematocrit can signal underlying issues ranging from benign (like mild dehydration) to serious (like bone marrow disorders).<Cite id="medlineplus-hematocrit" />

## Normal Hematocrit Ranges

Normal adult hematocrit is roughly 37% to 48% for men and 34% to 43% for women according to MedlinePlus, and 40% to 54% for men and 36% to 48% for women in a StatPearls review. Newborns run much higher. Always compare your number with the range printed on your own lab report.<Cite id="medlineplus-hematocrit" /><Cite id="statpearls-2024-cbc" />

| Group | MedlinePlus | StatPearls review |
|---|---|---|
| Men, adult | 37% to 48% | 40% to 54% |
| Women, adult | 34% to 43% | 36% to 48% |
| Newborns | 45% to 61% | not listed |
| Infants | 32% to 42% | not listed |

MedlinePlus notes that normal value ranges vary slightly among labs, and that some labs use different measurements or test different samples.<Cite id="medlineplus-hematocrit" /> The StatPearls figures are the "usual" reference interval it describes for adults.<Cite id="statpearls-2024-cbc" />

Other groups have their own reference points:

| Group | Typical hematocrit |
|---|---|
| Pregnant women | 33% or higher (32% or higher in the second trimester), lowered by plasma expansion |
| Teens, 12-19 | about 33-44% (girls), 37-50% (boys) |
| Children, 6-11 | about 33-44% |

The teen and child figures are the middle 95% of values in a US national survey, and the pregnancy figures are CDC anemia cut-offs.<Cite id="nchs-2005-hematology-reference" /><Cite id="cdc-1998-iron-deficiency" />

Women naturally have lower hematocrit than men, largely because testosterone stimulates red blood cell production.<Cite id="murphy-2014-sex-difference-hemoglobin" /><Cite id="bachman-2014-testosterone-erythropoiesis" /> Pregnancy lowers hematocrit further through plasma expansion.<Cite id="horowitz-2013-anemia-pregnancy" /> Altitude matters too: someone living at elevation may run a higher hematocrit and still be healthy.<Cite id="cdc-1998-iron-deficiency" />

## What Counts as High Hematocrit?

Any result above your lab's upper limit is flagged as high, but guidelines use a few specific numbers. Above 48% in men is a reason for caution before testosterone therapy, above 49% in men or 48% in women is one criterion for polycythemia vera, and above 54% is where testosterone is usually paused.<Cite id="bhasin-2018-testosterone-guideline" /><Cite id="icc-2022-myeloid" />

| Hematocrit result | What it is associated with | Where the number comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Above your lab's upper limit (about 48% to 54% in men, 43% to 48% in women) | Flagged as high; hydration and a repeat test are usually considered first | MedlinePlus and StatPearls reference ranges |
| Above 48% in men (above 50% at high altitude) | A relative reason not to start testosterone therapy | Endocrine Society guideline, 2018 |
| Above 49% in men or above 48% in women | Meets the hematocrit part of one major criterion for polycythemia vera, which also requires other findings | 2022 International Consensus Classification (same thresholds as WHO 2016) |
| Above 54% | Stop testosterone therapy until hematocrit falls to a safe level | Endocrine Society guideline, 2018 |

None of these numbers is a diagnosis on its own.<Cite id="medlineplus-hematocrit" /><Cite id="statpearls-2024-cbc" /><Cite id="bhasin-2018-testosterone-guideline" /><Cite id="icc-2022-myeloid" /> A hematocrit just above range after a hot day or a missed drink is a very different finding from one that stays high across repeat tests alongside a high hemoglobin, which is the pattern clinicians look into further.

## Is Your Specific Hematocrit Normal? (35, 40, 45, 50, 55)

The same number can be normal for one person and a flag for another, depending on sex, age, hydration and which reference range your lab uses. Here is how the most-searched values read against the MedlinePlus and StatPearls adult ranges, from 35% up to 55%.<Cite id="medlineplus-hematocrit" /><Cite id="statpearls-2024-cbc" />

**Hematocrit of 35%**: Below both male ranges, and around the bottom of the female ranges (inside MedlinePlus's 34% to 43%, just under StatPearls's 36%). For a man, 35% is low and often points toward anemia, most often [iron deficiency](/blog/low-ferritin-normal-hemoglobin), which iron studies can confirm.<Cite id="van-vranken-2010-microcytosis" /> For a woman it is borderline-low (the CDC's anemia cut-off for adult women is 35.7%), common with heavy periods or low iron.<Cite id="cdc-1998-iron-deficiency" /> The red-cell indices help show which kind of anemia it is; see [what MCV and MCH mean](/blog/mcv-mch-blood-test-meaning).

**Hematocrit of 40%**: Normal for women on both ranges, and normal to low-normal for men (inside MedlinePlus's range, right at the 40% floor of the StatPearls range). If it has drifted down from a previously higher value, it is worth watching.

**Hematocrit of 45%**: Mid-range for men on both references. For women it is above the MedlinePlus upper limit of 43% but inside the StatPearls range of 36% to 48%, so how it is flagged depends on your lab.

**Hematocrit of 50%**: For women, above both ranges. For men, above the MedlinePlus range but inside the StatPearls range of 40% to 54%. It is also just above the 49% level used as a polycythemia vera criterion in men and above the 48% level the Endocrine Society treats as a relative reason not to start testosterone.<Cite id="icc-2022-myeloid" /><Cite id="bhasin-2018-testosterone-guideline" /> Common drivers to rule out are dehydration on the day of the draw, sleep apnea, smoking and testosterone therapy.<Cite id="mithoowani-2020-erythrocytosis" />

**Hematocrit of 55%**: Above the normal range for both sexes and into polycythemia territory. Dehydration is usually ruled out first, then chronic low oxygen, smoking, testosterone therapy or a bone marrow condition such as polycythemia vera.<Cite id="mithoowani-2020-erythrocytosis" /> It is above the 54% level at which the Endocrine Society advises stopping testosterone therapy until hematocrit falls, and polycythemia vera is managed to keep hematocrit below 45%, because thicker blood is associated with higher clotting and cardiovascular risk.<Cite id="bhasin-2018-testosterone-guideline" /><Cite id="tefferi-2023-polycythemia-vera" /><Cite id="marchioli-2013-cyto-pv" /> This is a number to discuss with your clinician rather than dismiss.

## Common Causes of High Hematocrit

The most common explanations are temporary or lifestyle-related: dehydration, smoking, living at altitude, untreated sleep apnea and testosterone therapy. Less often, lung or heart disease, a kidney tumor that releases EPO, or a bone marrow condition called polycythemia vera may be involved, which is why a persistent high result is followed up.<Cite id="medlineplus-hematocrit" /><Cite id="mithoowani-2020-erythrocytosis" />

A hematocrit above the normal range is called **polycythemia** or **erythrocytosis**, an elevated red blood cell count relative to plasma.<Cite id="mithoowani-2020-erythrocytosis" />

**Dehydration (a common cause)**
Losing fluids without replacing them concentrates your blood, raising hematocrit artificially. A blood test after hard exercise, heavy sweating, or inadequate water intake often shows elevated hematocrit. MedlinePlus lists dehydration, for example from severe diarrhea, among the causes of high hematocrit, and the value usually settles once you are rehydrated.<Cite id="medlineplus-hematocrit" /><Cite id="billett-1990-hemoglobin-hematocrit" />

**Polycythemia Vera**
This myeloproliferative disorder causes the bone marrow to overproduce red blood cells autonomously. The WHO and the 2022 International Consensus Classification both use hemoglobin above 16.5 g/dL or hematocrit above 49% in men (above 16.0 g/dL or 48% in women) as one major criterion, which has to be combined with other findings such as bone marrow results and, in most cases, a JAK2 mutation.<Cite id="icc-2022-myeloid" /><Cite id="arber-2016-who-myeloid" /><Cite id="khoury-2022-who-myeloid" /> People with polycythemia vera are at increased risk for blood clots, stroke, and myocardial infarction.<Cite id="tefferi-2023-polycythemia-vera" /><Cite id="marchioli-2013-cyto-pv" />

**Chronic Hypoxia**
Your body compensates for low oxygen by producing more RBCs. Causes include:<Cite id="mithoowani-2020-erythrocytosis" />
- High altitude residence (the effect starts from around 3,000 feet)<Cite id="cdc-1998-iron-deficiency" />
- Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and scarring of the lungs (pulmonary fibrosis)<Cite id="medlineplus-hematocrit" />
- Congenital heart disease and right-sided heart failure (cor pulmonale)<Cite id="medlineplus-hematocrit" />
- Sleep apnea (especially untreated)

**Testosterone Replacement Therapy or Anabolic Steroids**
Testosterone stimulates erythropoietin (EPO), which signals the bone marrow to produce more RBCs.<Cite id="bachman-2014-testosterone-erythropoiesis" /> In trials in middle-aged and older men, those on testosterone were nearly four times as likely as men on placebo to have a hematocrit above 50%, and it was the most frequent side effect in that analysis, so clinicians usually monitor hematocrit during therapy.<Cite id="calof-2005-testosterone-adverse-events" /><Cite id="bhasin-2018-testosterone-guideline" />

**Smoking**
Cigarette smoking is listed among the causes of high hematocrit; carbon monoxide exposure lowers the oxygen your blood can carry, and the body may respond by making more red cells.<Cite id="medlineplus-hematocrit" /><Cite id="mithoowani-2020-erythrocytosis" />

**EPO-Secreting Tumors**
Rarely, a kidney tumor (renal cell carcinoma) or another tumor releases EPO, which may drive extra red cell production.<Cite id="medlineplus-hematocrit" /><Cite id="mithoowani-2020-erythrocytosis" />

**Why it matters:** High hematocrit thickens blood, increasing viscosity. In a large population study, each 5-point rise in hematocrit raised the risk of venous blood clots (such as DVT) by about 25%, with higher estimates in men.<Cite id="braekkan-2010-hematocrit-vte" /> In polycythemia vera, a randomized trial found that keeping hematocrit at 45-50% rather than below 45% led to more cardiovascular deaths and major blood clots,<Cite id="marchioli-2013-cyto-pv" /> whereas for secondary erythrocytosis there is no definitive evidence of extra clot risk.<Cite id="mithoowani-2020-erythrocytosis" />

## What Low Hematocrit Means

Low hematocrit means red blood cells make up less of your blood than your lab's range, below about 37% to 40% in men or 34% to 36% in women depending on the reference used. It usually reflects anemia, most often from low iron, and is read together with hemoglobin, MCV and ferritin.<Cite id="medlineplus-hematocrit" /><Cite id="statpearls-2024-cbc" />

Low hematocrit is called **anemia**—insufficient red blood cells to meet oxygen demands. It's among the most common blood abnormalities.<Cite id="gbd-2023-anaemia" />

### Causes of Low Hematocrit

**Iron Deficiency Anemia**
Iron is essential for hemoglobin synthesis. Without it, RBCs are small (microcytic) and contain less hemoglobin. Causes include heavy menstrual bleeding, GI bleeding (ulcers, hemorrhoids, occult bleeding), inadequate dietary intake, and poor absorption.<Cite id="van-vranken-2010-microcytosis" /><Cite id="pasricha-2021-iron-deficiency" /> This is the most common cause globally.<Cite id="gbd-2023-anaemia" /> Ferritin (your iron stores) often falls long before hemoglobin does, which is why [normal hemoglobin can still hide iron deficiency](/blog/low-ferritin-normal-hemoglobin).<Cite id="pasricha-2021-iron-deficiency" />

**Nutritional Deficiencies**
- **Vitamin B12 deficiency:** RBCs are large (macrocytic); seen in pernicious anemia, vegans without supplementation, or those with absorption issues<Cite id="ods-vitamin-b12" />
- **Folate deficiency:** Also causes macrocytic anemia; associated with poor diet, alcohol abuse, or certain medications<Cite id="kaferle-2009-macrocytosis" />
- **Vitamin C deficiency:** Vitamin C helps absorb iron from plant foods, and severe deficiency (scurvy) can lead to iron deficiency anemia<Cite id="ods-vitamin-c" />

**Chronic Disease Anemia**
Kidney disease, rheumatoid arthritis, cancer, and chronic infections reduce EPO production or increase inflammation, suppressing RBC synthesis.<Cite id="weiss-2019-anemia-of-inflammation" />

**Kidney Disease**
The kidneys produce erythropoietin. Advanced chronic kidney disease causes EPO deficiency and anemia.<Cite id="niddk-anemia-ckd" />

**Blood Loss**
Acute hemorrhage (trauma, surgery) or chronic bleeding (GI ulcers, heavy periods) deplete RBC reserves faster than the bone marrow can replenish them.<Cite id="brill-2000-normocytic-anemia" /><Cite id="van-vranken-2010-microcytosis" />

**Bone Marrow Disorders**
Aplastic anemia, myelodysplastic syndromes, and leukemia impair RBC production.<Cite id="medlineplus-hematocrit" />

**Hemolysis (RBC Destruction)**
Conditions like hereditary spherocytosis, autoimmune hemolytic anemia, or sickle cell disease destroy RBCs faster than they're replaced.<Cite id="medlineplus-hematocrit" />

**Pregnancy**
Plasma volume expands by close to 50% during pregnancy, more than red cell mass does, intentionally lowering hematocrit.<Cite id="aguree-2019-plasma-volume-pregnancy" /><Cite id="horowitz-2013-anemia-pregnancy" /> This is normal and protective, part of the body's preparation for blood loss at delivery.<Cite id="horowitz-2013-anemia-pregnancy" />

**Key insight:** Low hematocrit causes fatigue, dyspnea on exertion, dizziness, and reduced exercise capacity.<Cite id="who-anaemia-factsheet" /> By WHO criteria, anemia is severe once hemoglobin falls below 8 g/dL.<Cite id="who-2024-hemoglobin-cutoffs" />

## Hematocrit vs. Hemoglobin: How They Differ

Hematocrit is the share of your blood volume taken up by red cells, in percent, while hemoglobin is the amount of oxygen-carrying protein, in g/dL. The two usually rise and fall together, so a high hematocrit with a normal hemoglobin, or the reverse, is a pattern worth asking your clinician about.<Cite id="statpearls-2024-cbc" />

| Measure | What It Is | Units | Typical Value |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hematocrit** | % of blood volume as RBCs | % | 45% (men) |
| **Hemoglobin** | Oxygen-carrying protein in RBCs | g/dL | 15 g/dL (men) |

Hemoglobin is the protein that binds oxygen. Hematocrit is the volume occupied by the cells containing that protein. Normally, they move together—a low [hemoglobin level](/blog/hemoglobin-14-normal) usually means low hematocrit.<Cite id="nchs-2005-hematology-reference" /> But discrepancies occur, and because hemoglobin divided by hematocrit gives the MCHC (how densely the cells are filled with hemoglobin), they point to the cells' hemoglobin content rather than their size:<Cite id="statpearls-2024-cbc" />

- **Hematocrit high relative to hemoglobin:** cells are underfilled with hemoglobin (a low MCHC), the hypochromic pattern of iron deficiency<Cite id="statpearls-2024-cbc" />
- **Hemoglobin high relative to hematocrit:** cells are unusually dense (a high MCHC), as in hereditary spherocytosis<Cite id="sarma-1990-red-cell-indices" />
- **Both low:** True anemia requiring investigation

## When to Talk to Your Clinician About High Hematocrit

A high result is worth a conversation if it stays above your lab's range on a repeat test while you are well hydrated, if you are on testosterone, or if it comes with symptoms. Chest pain, sudden vision changes or signs of a blood clot need urgent care rather than waiting for a routine visit.<Cite id="bhasin-2018-testosterone-guideline" /><Cite id="tefferi-2023-polycythemia-vera" />

**High hematocrit is usually looked into further if:**
- It stays above your lab's range on a repeat test and you are not dehydrated
- You have symptoms such as headache, itching, blurred vision or chest pain<Cite id="tefferi-2023-polycythemia-vera" /><Cite id="mithoowani-2020-erythrocytosis" />
- You are on testosterone replacement<Cite id="bhasin-2018-testosterone-guideline" />
- You have risk factors for blood clots

**Low hematocrit is usually looked into further if:**
- It is below your lab's range, especially if it is a new change
- You have fatigue, shortness of breath, dizziness or reduced exercise tolerance
- It is dropping over time

Depending on the picture, your clinician may suggest iron studies, a reticulocyte count, B12 and folate levels, kidney function tests or, less often, specialist hematology tests.<Cite id="van-vranken-2010-microcytosis" /><Cite id="kaferle-2009-macrocytosis" />

## Hematocrit and Cardiovascular Risk

A higher hematocrit makes blood thicker, and in a large Norwegian population study each 5-point rise in hematocrit was associated with about a 25% higher risk of venous blood clots, with stronger links in men. That is why a persistently high result is usually followed up rather than ignored.<Cite id="braekkan-2010-hematocrit-vte" />

Elevated hematocrit (especially above 50%) increases blood viscosity, raising the blood's resistance to flow.<Cite id="lowe-1987-blood-rheology" /> This:
- Elevates clotting risk<Cite id="braekkan-2010-hematocrit-vte" />
- Can cause headaches, visual disturbances, or light-headedness in polycythemia vera<Cite id="mithoowani-2020-erythrocytosis" />

Clinical guidelines call for checking hematocrit in men on testosterone replacement (at baseline, at 3-6 months, then yearly) and pausing therapy if it rises above 54%. If hematocrit climbs too high, options include stopping temporarily and restarting at a reduced dose, or therapeutic phlebotomy (blood removal).<Cite id="bhasin-2018-testosterone-guideline" />

Conversely, very low hematocrit (anemia) may make the heart work harder to deliver oxygen.<Cite id="medlineplus-anemia" /> Your clinician will judge where your own result should sit.

## Everyday Factors That May Affect Hematocrit

Some drivers of a high hematocrit are within your control: hydration before the draw, smoking and untreated sleep apnea. For a low hematocrit, iron, B12 and folate status matter most. Changes to testosterone therapy or supplements are decisions to make with your clinician, not on your own.<Cite id="mithoowani-2020-erythrocytosis" /><Cite id="pasricha-2021-iron-deficiency" />

**If hematocrit runs high:**
- Drink normally before a blood draw so the result is not skewed by dehydration
- If you are on testosterone, ask your clinician whether your dose or monitoring schedule needs a review<Cite id="bhasin-2018-testosterone-guideline" />
- Ask about a sleep apnea assessment if you snore heavily or wake unrefreshed
- Stopping smoking removes one of the common drivers

**If hematocrit runs low:**
- Ask about [iron studies](/blog/ferritin-30-low-normal) (ferritin, iron, TIBC, transferrin saturation)
- Ask whether [B12 and folate levels](/blog/vitamin-b12-level-300) are worth checking
- Unexplained low iron is usually followed by a search for blood loss<Cite id="pasricha-2021-iron-deficiency" />
- Iron-rich foods (red meat, legumes, dark leafy greens) and vitamin C with plant-based meals support iron intake<Cite id="ods-vitamin-c" />

## Track Hematocrit Over Time

A single hematocrit is a snapshot, and hydration alone can move it. Looking at several results side by side shows whether a high value is a one-off or a steady climb, which is what your clinician will want to know before deciding on next steps.<Cite id="billett-1990-hemoglobin-hematocrit" />
