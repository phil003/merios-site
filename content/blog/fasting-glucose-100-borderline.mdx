## Is Fasting Glucose 100 mg/dL Bad?

**Fasting glucose 100 mg/dL sits exactly at the prediabetes threshold.** Below 100 = "normal" by ADA criteria. 100–125 = impaired fasting glucose (prediabetes). 126+ = diabetes. (The WHO starts impaired fasting glucose at 110 mg/dL, so outside the US a 100 may still be reported as normal.)<Cite id="ada-2025-diagnosis" /> So 100 is the wake-up call number — early, often reversible, but not something to ignore.<Cite id="tabak-2012-prediabetes" /> A continuous glucose monitor shows the same story minute by minute — covered in [using a CGM without diabetes](/blog/cgm-without-diabetes).

## Where Does 100 mg/dL Fall?

| Category | Fasting Glucose (mg/dL) |
|----------|--------------------------|
| Optimal (longevity) | 72–85 |
| Normal (clinical) | 70–99 |
| **Your value (100 mg/dL)** | **Impaired fasting glucose** |
| Prediabetes | 100–125 |
| Diabetes | &gt;=126 |

The ADA's "normal" cutoff (99 mg/dL) is not a cliff edge. In pooled data from 97 cohorts, fasting glucose above 100 mg/dL — but not 70–100 — was linked to a higher risk of death,<Cite id="seshasai-2011-erfc-death" /> while future diabetes risk still rose within the normal range, from about 87 mg/dL, in a large cohort of young men.<Cite id="tirosh-2005-normal-fasting-glucose" /> The longevity-focused target is **&lt;85 mg/dL** if possible.

## Why 100 mg/dL Is the Critical Window

At 100, you're at the **threshold of reversibility**. Research is clear:

- The Diabetes Prevention Program (NEJM 2002) showed lifestyle intervention (7% weight loss + 150 min/week activity) reduced progression to type 2 diabetes by **58% over 3 years** — better than metformin (31%).<Cite id="knowler-2002-dpp" />
- Lifestyle change moves the number, but gradually: in trials of people with prediabetes, resistance or interval training programs of 12+ weeks lowered fasting glucose by about 5–7 mg/dL on average.<Cite id="bennasar-veny-2023-exercise-prediabetes" />
- [Past 110](#fasting-glucose-110-mg-dl), the trajectory accelerates: in one large health-plan cohort, impaired fasting glucose that started at 110–125 progressed to diabetes at 5.6% a year, versus 1.3% for 100–109.<Cite id="nichols-2007-ifg-progression" />

## What to Do at 100 mg/dL — Order by Impact

1. **Reduce refined carbs and added sugars** — single biggest dietary lever. Swap white bread, pasta, sugar-sweetened drinks for whole grains, legumes, water/unsweetened tea.<Cite id="evert-2019-nutrition-consensus" />
2. **Strength training 2–3×/week** — builds muscle = better glucose disposal.<Cite id="srikanthan-2011-muscle-mass" /> In trials in type 2 diabetes, combining it with cardio beat either one alone for glucose control.<Cite id="schwingshackl-2014-training-nma" />
3. **10-minute post-meal walks** — in a trial in type 2 diabetes, a 10-minute walk after each meal left post-meal glucose 12% lower than one daily 30-minute walk did (22% lower after dinner). Trivial effort, real impact.<Cite id="reynolds-2016-post-meal-walking" />
4. **Sleep 7+ hours** — a single night of 4 hours' sleep cut insulin sensitivity by about 25% in healthy adults.<Cite id="donga-2010-sleep-insulin" /><Cite id="cdc-sleep" />
5. **Drop 5–10% body weight** if overweight — directly improves insulin sensitivity.<Cite id="magkos-2016-weight-loss" />

## Fasting Glucose 110 mg/dL

110 is mid-range prediabetes, past the early edge of 100 and exactly where the WHO's narrower impaired fasting glucose range (110–125 mg/dL) begins.<Cite id="ada-2025-diagnosis" /> A high fasting number usually means the liver is releasing glucose overnight faster than insulin can clear it, which is a different mechanism from the after-meal spike, driven more by insulin resistance in muscle.<Cite id="abdul-ghani-2006-ifg-igt" />

It also often disagrees with A1C: in US national survey data, only about a quarter of adults with impaired fasting glucose also had a prediabetic A1C (5.7–6.4%).<Cite id="mann-2010-a1c-ifg" /> If your fasting glucose is 110 but your A1C is normal, the overnight number may be the early signal; if A1C is high too, daytime spikes are adding up. [What 5.7%, 5.8% and 6.0% mean](/blog/hba1c-5-7-pre-diabetic) covers the A1C side.

Two habits target the overnight number specifically. In a small trial in men with prediabetes, eating within an early 6-hour window (dinner before 3 p.m.) improved insulin sensitivity even without weight loss,<Cite id="sutton-2018-etrf" /> and in a crossover trial, the same dinner eaten at 10 p.m. instead of 6 p.m. kept glucose higher overnight.<Cite id="gu-2020-late-dinner" /> Everything in the list above applies too.

## Don't Test Glucose Alone — The Combo That Matters

Fasting glucose tells you what your blood sugar IS at one moment. But the bigger signal is **what your body is doing to keep it there**:

- **Fasting insulin** (no universal reference range — assays differ between labs) — high insulin with normal glucose = your pancreas is working overtime<Cite id="marcovina-2007-insulin-assays" /><Cite id="defronzo-2009-banting" />
- **HOMA-IR** = fasting glucose × fasting insulin / 405 (the model defines 1.0 as normal)<Cite id="matthews-1985-homa" /><Cite id="wallace-2004-homa" />
- **HbA1c** (optimal &lt;5.5%)<Cite id="selvin-2010-a1c-aric" /> — your [90-day glucose average](/tools/a1c-calculator)

If you do not have a fasting insulin result, the [TyG index calculator](/tools/tyg-index-calculator) estimates insulin resistance from triglycerides and glucose alone.

Use the [HOMA-IR calculator](/tools/homa-ir-calculator) to compute your insulin resistance score from the same fasted draw. A HOMA-IR above 2.5 alongside a fasting glucose of 100 mg/dL points toward insulin resistance, and scores between 2.0 and 2.5 are a gray zone. Cutoffs vary by population — published thresholds run from under 2 to about 3.5 — so bring the number to your physician rather than reading it as a diagnosis.<Cite id="gayoso-diz-2013-homa-cutoffs" />

## Recheck Schedule

- **Week 12**: recheck fasting glucose, fasting insulin, HbA1c after lifestyle change
- If FG dropped to &lt;90: keep going, you're winning
- If FG is still 100+ at week 12: tighten the protocol and ask your physician whether metformin fits — the ADA suggests considering it in higher-risk prediabetes, for example with a BMI of 35+, fasting glucose of 110+, A1C of 6.0%+ or prior gestational diabetes<Cite id="ada-2025-prevention" />
