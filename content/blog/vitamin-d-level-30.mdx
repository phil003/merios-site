## Is Vitamin D 30 ng/mL Normal?

**Vitamin D 30 ng/mL is "normal" by lab ranges and adequate by the IOM's standard.**<Cite id="ods-vitamin-d" /> Many labs list 30–100 as "normal" — the range the Endocrine Society's 2011 guideline called sufficient — and some integrative doctors target 40–60 as optimal, though trials have not shown added benefit from going higher.<Cite id="holick-2011-endocrine-society" /><Cite id="demay-2024-endocrine-society" /> A level of 30 puts you at the lower end of the lab range but won't cause any immediate harm.

## Where Does 30 ng/mL Fall?

| Category | Range (ng/mL) |
|----------|----------------|
| Deficient | &lt;20 |
| Insufficient | 20–29 |
| Normal (lab range) | 30–100 |
| **Your value (30)** | **Bottom of the lab range** |
| Optimal (functional) | 40–60 |
| Excessive (rare) | &gt;150 |

The deficient and insufficient cut-offs above are the Endocrine Society's 2011 definitions; in 2024 the Society withdrew them, and the IOM treats 20 ng/mL and above as adequate.<Cite id="mccartney-2024-epistemic-humility" /><Cite id="ods-vitamin-d" />

### What This Means

At 30, you're not deficient, and you won't have acute symptoms like bone pain or muscle weakness.<Cite id="ods-vitamin-d" /> Whether higher levels add protection — stronger bones, better immune function, improved mood, and lower inflammation — is unproven: in the VITAL trial, 2,000 IU daily raised average levels from about 30 to about 42 ng/mL without reducing cancer, cardiovascular events, fractures, or depression.<Cite id="manson-2019-vital" /><Cite id="leboff-2022-vital-fractures" /><Cite id="okereke-2020-vital-dep" />

If your report reads 75 nmol/L rather than 30 ng/mL, that is the same result: outside the US, 25-OH vitamin D is reported in nmol/L, and dividing by 2.496 gives ng/mL.<Cite id="ginde-2009-urti" /> The [nmol/L to mg/dL conversion table](/tools/lpa-unit-converter) covers vitamin D alongside the other markers labs report in two different unit systems.

## Why "Normal" Isn't Always Optimal

Vitamin D thresholds were historically set around bone health. The IOM's threshold for bone health is [around 20](/blog/vitamin-d-level-20), and rickets (a severe bone disease) is tied to levels below 12.<Cite id="ross-2011-iom-dri" /><Cite id="ods-vitamin-d" /> Some clinicians argue that levels of 40–60 offer better protection for:

- **Bone health:** Higher vitamin D correlates with better bone density, but in trials supplementation has not prevented fractures.<Cite id="bischoff-ferrari-2004-bmd" /><Cite id="bolland-2018-musculoskeletal" />
- **Immune function:** Vitamin D modulates immune cells; in US survey data, people below 30 ng/mL reported more respiratory infections than those at 30 or above.<Cite id="aranow-2011-immune" /><Cite id="ginde-2009-urti" />
- **Mood and mental health:** Low vitamin D is associated with depression, but supplementation did not prevent depression in a large trial.<Cite id="anglin-2013-depression" /><Cite id="okereke-2020-vital-dep" />
- **Inflammation:** Genetic studies suggest low vitamin D raises CRP, but only in the deficiency range; the effect levels off around 20 ng/mL.<Cite id="zhou-2023-vitamin-d-crp" />

At 30, you're protecting yourself from the worst outcomes; whether higher levels add benefit has not been established in clinical trials.<Cite id="demay-2024-endocrine-society" /> For the closely related question of whether 30 is low-normal or insufficient, see [is a vitamin D of 30 enough?](/blog/vitamin-d-30-low)

## Risk Factors for Low Vitamin D

You might have 30 because of:

- **Limited sun exposure:** If you live in northern climates, work indoors, or avoid sun (wise for skin cancer prevention, but vitamin D does suffer).<Cite id="ods-vitamin-d" />
- **Dark skin:** Melanin reduces vitamin D synthesis. People with darker skin tones need considerably more sun exposure (about six times the UV dose in one small study) or supplementation to reach the same levels.<Cite id="clemens-1982-skin-pigment" />
- **Malabsorption disorders:** Celiac disease, Crohn's disease, and ulcerative colitis reduce vitamin D absorption.<Cite id="ods-vitamin-d" />
- **Older age:** Skin becomes less efficient at vitamin D synthesis with age.<Cite id="maclaughlin-1985-aging-skin" />
- **Dietary gaps:** Fatty fish, egg yolks, and fortified dairy are vitamin D sources; vegan diets often fall short without supplementation.<Cite id="crowe-2011-epic-oxford" />

These same factors — malabsorption, vegan diets, and aging — often cause [low B12 alongside low vitamin D](/blog/vitamin-d-b12-deficiency-together).<Cite id="ods-vitamin-b12" />

## What to Do Next

1. **Start supplementation:** 2,000–4,000 IU of vitamin D3 daily stays within the 4,000 IU upper limit and will raise your level, though the 2024 Endocrine Society guideline suggests healthy adults under 75 don't need more than the RDA (600–800 IU) to prevent disease.<Cite id="ods-vitamin-d" /><Cite id="demay-2024-endocrine-society" /> Take it with a fat-containing meal for better absorption.<Cite id="dawson-hughes-2015-dietary-fat" /> Some people pair D3 with [vitamin K2](/blog/vitamin-k2-d3-together), which activates proteins involved in calcium handling, though a trial of the combination did not slow arterial or valve calcification.<Cite id="ods-vitamin-k" /><Cite id="diederichsen-2022-avadec" /> Ensure [adequate magnesium](/blog/magnesium-deficiency-symptoms-women), which your body needs to activate vitamin D.<Cite id="uwitonze-2018-magnesium-vitamin-d" />

2. **Retest in 12 weeks:** After 12 weeks of supplementation, get your level rechecked. Functional medicine practitioners aim for 40–60 ng/mL; the 2024 Endocrine Society guideline sets no target level at all.<Cite id="demay-2024-endocrine-society" /> If you're moving slowly, your doctor might suggest a higher dose (doses above the 4,000 IU upper limit belong under medical supervision).<Cite id="ods-vitamin-d" />

3. **Increase sun exposure (safely):** 5–30 minutes of midday sun at least twice a week supports vitamin D synthesis — without overdoing it.<Cite id="ods-vitamin-d" /> Sunscreen blocks UV-B in lab tests, but in real-life use it has little effect on vitamin D levels, so vitamin D is no reason to skip it.<Cite id="neale-2019-sunscreen" />

4. **Eat vitamin D-rich foods:** Salmon, mackerel, sardines, egg yolks, and fortified milk and plant-based milks all help. But it's hard to get much vitamin D from food alone: average US intakes from food are only about 170–200 IU a day.<Cite id="ods-vitamin-d" />
